He instructed his son as well: “I warn you to run
along a middle course, Icarus, lest, if you go too low,
the water weigh down your feathers, or, if too high, the fire scorch them.
Fly between the two. Do not watch Boötes
or Helice or Orion’s drawn sword:
take your way with me as guide.” At the same time he delivered the rules of flight
and fitted the unfamiliar wings to his shoulders.
Amid the work and the warnings the old man’s cheeks grew wet,
and the father’s hands trembled. He gave his son kisses
not to be given again, and, lifted on his wings,
flew ahead and feared for his companion, like a bird which from a high
nest has led its tender young into the air;
he urged him to follow and taught the destructive art,
and moved his own wings and looked back at his son’s.
