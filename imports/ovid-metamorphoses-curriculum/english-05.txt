Meanwhile Daedalus, hating Crete and his long exile
and touched by love of his birthplace,
was shut in by the sea. “Though he may bar the lands,” he said, “and the waves,
surely the sky is open; we shall go that way.
Minos may possess everything, but he does not possess the air.”
He spoke, and sent his mind into unknown arts
and altered nature. For he arranged feathers in order,
starting with the smallest, a longer one following a shorter,
so that you might think they had grown on a slope. Thus once a rustic
pipe rises gradually from unequal reeds.
Then he bound the middles with thread and the bottoms with wax,
and bent those arranged in this way with a slight curve,
so that he might imitate real birds. The boy Icarus stood
beside him and, unaware that he was handling his own dangers,
now, with smiling face, caught the feathers that the wandering breeze
had stirred, now softened the yellow wax with his thumb,
and with his play hindered his father’s wondrous
work. After the finishing touch had been placed upon the undertaking,
the craftsman himself balanced his body on the twin wings
and hung suspended in the stirred air.
