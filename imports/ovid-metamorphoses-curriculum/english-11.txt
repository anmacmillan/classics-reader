When he returned, he sought the image of his girl
and, bending over the couch, gave it kisses: it seemed warm.
He brought his mouth to it again and with his hands also tested the breast:
the ivory softened where it was touched and, its hardness laid aside,
sank beneath his fingers and yielded, as Hymettian wax in the sun
grows soft again and, worked by the thumb, is bent
into many forms and becomes useful through use itself.
While he is amazed and rejoices uncertainly and fears he is mistaken,
the lover again and again touches the object of his prayers with his hand.
It was a body: the veins tested by his thumb pulsed.
Then indeed the Cypriot hero found an abundance of
words with which to give thanks to Venus, and at last pressed lips
that were not false with his own; the maiden felt the kisses she was given
and blushed, and timidly raising her eyes to the light,
saw the sky and her lover at the same time.
The goddess attended the marriage she had made. And when the moon’s horns
had nine times come together into a full circle,
she bore Paphos, from whom the island takes its name.
