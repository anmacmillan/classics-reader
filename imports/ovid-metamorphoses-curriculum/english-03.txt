The rustic crowd forbade her. The goddess addressed those forbidding her thus:
“Why do you keep me from the water? The use of water belongs to all.
Nature has made neither sun nor air anyone’s property,
nor the clear waves: I have come to a public gift.
Yet I beg you as a suppliant to grant it. I was not preparing
to wash my limbs here or my weary body,
but to relieve my thirst. My mouth has no moisture as I speak,
my throat is parched, and scarcely can my voice pass through it.
A draught of water will be nectar to me, and I shall confess
that I received life with it: in the water you will have given me life.
Let these children too move you, who stretch their little arms
from my breast”: and by chance the children were stretching out their arms.
Whom could the goddess’s gentle words not have moved?
Yet they persisted in preventing her despite her prayers, and added threats
unless she went far away, and insults besides.
Nor was that enough: with their very feet and hands
they even churned up the pool and from the bottom of the water
spitefully stirred the soft mud this way and that by jumping.
