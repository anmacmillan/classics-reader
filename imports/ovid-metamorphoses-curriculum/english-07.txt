Someone, while catching fish with a trembling rod,
or a shepherd leaning on his staff, or a ploughman on his plough-handle,
saw them and was astonished; because they could travel through the sky,
he believed them to be gods. And now on the left-hand side was Juno’s
Samos (Delos and Paros had been left behind),
on the right were Lebinthos and honey-rich Calymne,
when the boy began to delight in his daring flight,
abandoned his guide and, drawn by desire for the sky,
drove his course higher. The nearness of the swift sun
softened the fragrant wax, the fastenings of the feathers.
The wax had melted; he beat his bare arms,
and, lacking his oarage, caught no air at all,
and his mouth, crying his father’s name, was swallowed
by the blue water, which took its name from him.
But his unhappy father, a father no longer, said, “Icarus,”
he said, “Icarus, where are you? In what region shall I search for you?”
He kept saying “Icarus”; he saw the feathers in the waves,
cursed his own arts, and placed the body in a tomb,
and the land was named after the one buried there.
