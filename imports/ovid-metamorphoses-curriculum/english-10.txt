The festival day of Venus, most celebrated throughout Cyprus,
had come, and heifers with gold laid on their curved horns
had fallen, struck on their snow-white necks,
and incense was smoking; when he had performed his duty, before the altars
he stood and timidly said, “If you gods can grant all things,
may my wife be, I pray”—not daring to say “the ivory maiden”—
“like my ivory girl,” Pygmalion said.
Golden Venus, since she herself attended her own festival,
understood what that prayer meant, and, as an omen of the friendly divinity,
the flame blazed up three times and drew its tip through the air.
