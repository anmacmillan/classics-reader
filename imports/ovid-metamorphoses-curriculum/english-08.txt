Since Pygmalion had seen them spending their lives in wickedness,
offended by the faults which nature had given in greatest number
to the female mind, he lived unmarried, without a wife,
and for a long time lacked a partner for his bed.
Meanwhile, with wondrous skill, successfully
he sculpted snow-white ivory and gave it a form with which no woman
can be born, and he conceived love for his own work.
It has the face of a real maiden, whom you would believe to live
and, if modesty did not prevent her, to wish to move:
so completely does his art conceal itself by its own art. He marvels and drinks in
with his heart the flames for the semblance of a body.
Often he brings testing hands to the work, to see whether it is
flesh or ivory; and he still does not admit it is ivory.
He gives it kisses and thinks they are returned, speaks to it and holds it,
and believes his fingers sink into the limbs he touches,
and fears that bruises may come upon the pressed limbs.
