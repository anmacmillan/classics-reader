After this he gathered his own soldiers, those who had come over to him, and anyone of the others who wished, and spoke as follows.
‘Soldiers, it is clear that Cyrus stands towards us just as we stand towards him;
for we are no longer his soldiers, since we are not following him, and he is no longer our paymaster.
That he thinks he is being wronged by us, however, I know;
so even though he is sending for me I am unwilling to go, above all because I am ashamed, since I know in my heart that I have deceived him in everything, and also because I am afraid that he will seize me and punish me for the wrongs he thinks I have done him.
So it seems to me that this is no time for us to sleep or to neglect ourselves, but to consider what we must do in these circumstances.
And as long as we stay here, it seems to me we must consider how we can stay most safely, and if we decide to leave at once, how we can leave most safely, and how we shall get provisions;
for without these neither a general nor a private soldier is of any use.
The man is very valuable to anyone whose friend he is, but a most dangerous enemy to anyone he is at war with, and he has forces of infantry, cavalry and ships which we all alike see and know about;
and indeed we do not seem to me to be encamped far from him.
So it is time for each to say what he thinks is best.’
Having said this, he stopped.
Then men stood up, some of their own accord, to say what they thought, others at his instigation, to point out how hopeless it would be, without Cyrus’ goodwill, either to stay or to leave.
One man, pretending to be eager to march back to Greece as quickly as possible, said that they should choose other generals as quickly as possible, if Clearchus did not want to lead them back;
that they should buy provisions (the market was in the barbarian army) and pack their baggage;
and that they should go to Cyrus and ask for ships, so that they could sail home;
and if he would not give them, that they should ask Cyrus for a guide who would lead them back through friendly country.
And if he would not even give a guide, they should form up in battle order as quickly as possible, and also send men ahead to seize the heights first, so that neither Cyrus nor the Cilicians could get there before them and occupy them, ‘since we have carried off many of their people and much of their property.’
That is what this man said;
after him Clearchus said just this much.
