Darius and Parysatis had two sons, the elder Artaxerxes and the younger Cyrus;
when Darius fell ill and suspected that the end of his life was near, he wanted both his sons to be with him.
Now the elder happened to be there already;
but he sends for Cyrus from the province of which he had made him satrap, and he had also appointed him general of all the forces that muster in the plain of Castolus.
So Cyrus goes up, taking Tissaphernes with him as a friend, and he went up with three hundred Greek hoplites, and as their commander Xenias of Parrhasia.
When Darius had died and Artaxerxes had been established as king, Tissaphernes slanders Cyrus to his brother, saying that he was plotting against him.
He believes it and arrests Cyrus, intending to put him to death;
but his mother begged him off and sends him back to his province.
When he had gone away, after being in danger and in disgrace, he plans how he will never again be in his brother's power but, if he can, will be king in his place.
Parysatis, his mother, was on Cyrus' side, since she loved him more than Artaxerxes, who was king.
Whoever came to him from the king, he sent them all away so disposed that they were more friendly to him than to the king.
And he also took care of the barbarians in his service, so that they would be fit to fight and well disposed towards him.
