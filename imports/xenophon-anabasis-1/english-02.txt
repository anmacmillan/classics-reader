He collected his Greek force as secretly as he could, so as to catch the king as unprepared as possible.
This, then, is how he went about the recruiting.
He ordered the commanders of all the garrisons he had in the cities to enlist as many Peloponnesian men as possible, and the best, on the grounds that Tissaphernes was plotting against the cities.
For the Ionian cities had originally belonged to Tissaphernes, given to him by the king, but at that time they had all gone over to Cyrus except Miletus;
in Miletus Tissaphernes, learning in advance that people were planning this same thing, to go over to Cyrus, put some of them to death and banished others.
Cyrus took the exiles under his protection, collected an army and besieged Miletus by land and by sea, and tried to restore the exiles.
And this in turn was another pretext for him to gather an army.
He sent to the king and asked that, since he was his brother, these cities should be given to him rather than that Tissaphernes should rule them, and his mother supported him in this;
so the king did not notice the plot against himself, but thought that Cyrus was spending money on his armies because he was at war with Tissaphernes;
and so he was not at all displeased that they were at war.
For Cyrus also sent the king the tribute that came in from the cities he happened to hold that had belonged to Tissaphernes.
Another army was being collected for him in the Chersonese opposite Abydos, in the following way.
Clearchus was a Spartan exile;
Cyrus met him, admired him, and gives him ten thousand darics.
He took the gold, raised an army with this money, and making the Chersonese his base he made war on the Thracians who live beyond the Hellespont and helped the Greeks;
so the cities on the Hellespont even willingly contributed money to him for the upkeep of his soldiers.
And this army too, maintained in this way, was being kept for him without anyone noticing.
Aristippus the Thessalian happened to be a guest-friend of his, and being hard pressed by his political opponents at home he comes to Cyrus and asks him for about two thousand mercenaries and three months' pay, saying that in this way he would get the better of his opponents.
Cyrus gives him about four thousand and six months' pay, and asks him not to come to terms with his opponents before he had consulted him.
In this way, again, the army in Thessaly was being maintained for him without anyone noticing.
He ordered Proxenus the Boeotian, who was a guest-friend of his, to take as many men as possible and join him, saying that he wanted to march against the Pisidians, because the Pisidians were causing trouble in his territory.
Sophaenetus of Stymphalus and Socrates the Achaean, who were also guest-friends of his, he ordered to come with as many men as possible, saying that he was going to make war on Tissaphernes together with the Milesian exiles.
And these men did so.
