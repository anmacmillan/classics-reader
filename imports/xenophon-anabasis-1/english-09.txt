‘Let none of you speak of me as the one who will hold this command;
for I see many reasons why I must not do this;
but that I shall obey the man you choose as far as I possibly can, so that you may know that I also know how to be commanded, as well as any man alive.’
After him another man stood up, pointing out the foolishness of the man who told them to ask for ships, as if Cyrus were making the expedition back home again, and pointing out how foolish it was to ask for a guide from the very man whose enterprise we are ruining.
‘And if we are going to trust the guide whom Cyrus gives us, what stops us from telling Cyrus to seize the heights for us as well?
For my part, I would hesitate to board the ships he might give us, for fear he might sink us with his triremes, and I would be afraid to follow the guide he might give, for fear he might lead us to a place from which there will be no way out;
and if I were leaving against Cyrus’ will, I would want to get away without his noticing;
which is impossible.
No, I say that these ideas are nonsense;
I think that suitable men should go to Cyrus with Clearchus and ask him what he wants to use us for;
and if the undertaking is similar to the one for which he used the mercenaries before, we too should follow and not be worse men than those who went up country with him before;
but if the undertaking turns out to be greater than the previous one, and more laborious and more dangerous, we should ask him either to persuade us and lead us on, or be persuaded himself and let us go as friends;
for in this way, if we follow, we would follow as his friends and willingly, and if we leave, we would leave in safety;
and whatever he says in reply, they should report it here;
and when we have heard it, we should decide what to do about it.’
This was agreed, and they chose men and sent them with Clearchus, and they asked Cyrus the questions the army had decided on.
He replied that he heard that Abrocomas, an enemy of his, was on the river Euphrates, twelve stages away;
it was against him, he said, that he wanted to go;
and if he was there, he said, he wanted to punish him, but if he fled, ‘we will consider the matter there.’
When the chosen men heard this, they reported it to the soldiers;
they suspected that he was leading them against the King, but all the same they decided to follow.
They asked for more pay;
and Cyrus promised to give all of them half as much again as they had been receiving before, three half-darics a month for each soldier instead of one daric;
but that he was leading them against the King, no one heard even then, at least not openly.
