There Cyrus and the army stayed for twenty days;
for the soldiers said they would not go any further;
for they already suspected that they were going against the King;
and they said they had not been hired for this.
Clearchus was the first to try to force his own soldiers to go on;
but they threw stones at him and at his pack animals whenever they began to move forward.
On that occasion Clearchus narrowly escaped being stoned to death, but later, when he realised that he would not be able to use force, he called an assembly of his own soldiers.
And first he stood there weeping for a long time;
and they, seeing this, were amazed and kept silent;
then he spoke as follows.
‘Soldiers, do not be surprised that I am distressed at the present situation.
For Cyrus became my guest-friend, and when I was an exile from my country he honoured me in various ways and gave me ten thousand darics;
when I received them, I did not put them aside for my own use or squander them on pleasure, but spent them on you.
And first I made war on the Thracians, and with you I took vengeance on behalf of Greece, driving them out of the Chersonese when they wanted to take away the land from the Greeks living there.
And when Cyrus summoned me, I took you and set out, so that if he needed anything I might help him in return for the good I had received from him.
But since you do not want to march on with me, I am forced either to betray you and keep Cyrus’ friendship, or to prove false to him and stay with you.
Whether I shall be doing what is right, I do not know, but at any rate I shall choose you, and with you I shall suffer whatever must be.
And no one shall ever say that I, having led Greeks into the land of the barbarians, betrayed the Greeks and chose the friendship of the barbarians; but since you are not willing to obey me, I shall follow with you and suffer whatever must be.
For I consider you to be my country, my friends and my allies, and with you, I think, I would be honoured wherever I am, but deprived of you I do not think I would be able either to help a friend or to ward off an enemy.
So be sure that I shall go wherever you go too.’
This is what he said;
and the soldiers, both his own and the others, when they heard him say that he would not march against the King, applauded him;
and from the troops of Xenias and Pasion more than two thousand took their arms and baggage and made camp beside Clearchus.
Cyrus, at a loss and distressed at this, sent for Clearchus;
Clearchus refused to go, but secretly, without the soldiers knowing, he sent him a messenger and told him to be of good heart, since this would be settled as it should be.
And he told him to keep sending for him;
but he himself said he would not go.
