From there he marched three stages, fifteen parasangs, to the river Euphrates, which was four stades wide;
and a large and prosperous city called Thapsacus was situated there.
There he stayed for five days.
And Cyrus sent for the generals of the Greeks and told them that the march would be against the Great King, to Babylon;
and he told them to tell this to the soldiers and to persuade them to follow.
They called an assembly and announced this;
the soldiers were angry with the generals and said that they had known this for a long time and kept it hidden, and they said they would not go unless someone gave them money, as had been given to those who had gone up earlier with Cyrus to Cyrus’ father, even though those men were not going to battle but Cyrus was going because his father had summoned him.
The generals reported this to Cyrus;
and he promised to give each man five minas of silver when they reached Babylon, and full pay until he brought the Greeks back to Ionia.
In this way the greater part of the Greek force was persuaded.
But Menon, before it was clear what the other soldiers would do, whether they would follow Cyrus or not, gathered his own troops apart from the others and spoke as follows.
‘Men, if you listen to me, without running any risk or doing any work you will be honoured by Cyrus above the other soldiers.
What, then, do I tell you to do?
Cyrus now needs the Greeks to follow him against the King;
so I say you should cross the river Euphrates before it is clear what answer the other Greeks will give Cyrus.
For if they vote to follow, you will be seen as responsible, since you began the crossing, and Cyrus will be grateful to you as the most eager of all and will repay you;
and he knows how to do that as well as anyone;
but if the others vote against it, we shall all go back again, but he will treat you, as the only ones who obeyed, as the most trustworthy, both for garrison posts and for commands of companies, and whatever else you may want, I know that you will get it from Cyrus as his friends.’
When they heard this, they did as he said and crossed before the others gave their answer.
When Cyrus learned that they had crossed, he was delighted, and sending Glus to the troops he said:
‘Men, I already commend you;
It will be my concern that you too will praise me, or else no longer consider me Cyrus.
So the soldiers, full of great hopes, prayed that he would succeed, and he was said to have sent Menon magnificent gifts as well.
After doing this he began to cross;
and the whole rest of the army followed him too.
And of those crossing the river, not one was wetted by the river above the chest.
The people of Thapsacus said that this river had never been crossable on foot except at that time, but only by boats, which Abrocomas had then burned as he went ahead, so that Cyrus should not cross.
So it seemed to be the work of the gods, and the river seemed plainly to have given way before Cyrus as the man who would be king.
From there he marched through Syria nine stages, fifty parasangs;
and they arrived at the river Araxes.
There were many villages there full of grain and wine.
There they stayed three days and gathered provisions.
