When he decided that it was now time to march up country, he gave as his pretext that he wanted to drive the Pisidians out of the country altogether;
and he gathers both his barbarian and his Greek troops, as if against them.
Then he also sends word to Clearchus to come with the whole army he had, and to Aristippus to make peace with his opponents at home and send him the army he had;
and to Xenias the Arcadian, who commanded the mercenary force in the cities for him, he sends word to come with the rest of the men, except as many as were enough to guard the citadels.
He also summoned the troops besieging Miletus and ordered the exiles to join his campaign, promising them that if he succeeded in what he was marching for, he would not stop until he had brought them back home.
They obeyed gladly;
for they trusted him;
and they took up their arms and arrived at Sardis.
Xenias arrived at Sardis with the men from the cities, about four thousand hoplites; Proxenus came with about fifteen hundred hoplites and five hundred light troops; Sophaenetus of Stymphalus with a thousand hoplites; Socrates the Achaean with about five hundred hoplites; and Pasion of Megara arrived with three hundred hoplites and three hundred peltasts;
he too, like Socrates, was one of those campaigning at Miletus.
These, then, came to join him at Sardis.
But Tissaphernes noticed all this, and judging the preparations to be too great to be aimed at the Pisidians, he travels to the king as fast as he could, with about five hundred horsemen.
And the king, when he heard from Tissaphernes about Cyrus' expedition, began preparations of his own.
Cyrus, with the troops I have mentioned, set out from Sardis;
and he marches through Lydia three stages, twenty-two parasangs, to the river Maeander.
Its width is two plethra;
and there was a bridge over it made of boats lashed together.
Having crossed it, he marches through Phrygia one stage, eight parasangs, to Colossae, an inhabited city, prosperous and large.
There he stayed seven days;
and Menon the Thessalian arrived with a thousand hoplites and five hundred peltasts, Dolopians, Aenianians and Olynthians.
