From there he marched through Arabia, keeping the river Euphrates on his right, five desert stages, thirty-five parasangs.
In this region the land was all a plain, level like the sea, and full of wormwood;
and whatever other brushwood or reed grew there was all fragrant like spices;
there was no tree there, but wild animals of every kind: very many wild asses, and many ostriches;
there were also bustards and gazelles;
the horsemen sometimes hunted these animals.
And the asses, whenever someone chased them, would run ahead and stand still;
for they ran much faster than the horses;
and again, whenever the horses came near, they did the same thing, and it was impossible to catch them unless the horsemen spread out at intervals and hunted them in relays.
The meat of those that were caught was like venison, but more tender.
But no one caught an ostrich;
those of the horsemen who chased one quickly gave up;
for it drew far away as it fled, running with its feet and lifting itself with its wings, using them like a sail.
But the bustards can be caught if one flushes them quickly;
for they fly only a short way, like partridges, and quickly tire.
Their meat was very delicious.
Marching through this country they arrived at the river Mascas, a plethron wide.
There was a large deserted city there, named Corsote;
it was surrounded all round by the Mascas.
