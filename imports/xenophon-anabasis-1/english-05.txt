From there he marched two stages, ten parasangs, to Tyriaeum, an inhabited city.
There he stayed three days.
And the Cilician queen is said to have asked Cyrus to show her the army;
so, wishing to show it, he holds a review of the Greeks and the barbarians on the plain.
He ordered the Greeks to draw up and take position in their usual battle order, each general marshalling his own men.
So they were drawn up four deep;
Menon and his men held the right wing, Clearchus and his men the left, and the other generals the centre.
Cyrus first inspected the barbarians;
they rode past drawn up in squadrons and companies;
then the Greeks, as he drove past in a chariot and the Cilician queen in a covered carriage.
They all had bronze helmets, crimson tunics and greaves, and their shields uncovered.
When he had driven past them all, he halted his chariot in front of the centre of the phalanx, sent Pigres the interpreter to the Greek generals and ordered them to lower their spears and advance with the whole phalanx.
They passed the order on to the soldiers;
and when the trumpet sounded, they lowered their spears and advanced.
Then, as they went forward faster and faster with a shout, the soldiers broke of their own accord into a run towards the tents, and great panic seized the barbarians: the Cilician queen fled in her carriage, and the people from the market left their wares and fled.
The Greeks came up to the tents laughing.
The Cilician queen, seeing the splendour and discipline of the army, was amazed.
And Cyrus was delighted to see the fear the Greeks struck into the barbarians.
From there he marched three stages, twenty parasangs, to Iconium, the last city of Phrygia.
There he stayed three days.
From there he marched through Lycaonia five stages, thirty parasangs.
He allowed the Greeks to plunder this country, since it was hostile territory.
From there Cyrus sends the Cilician queen back to Cilicia by the quickest road;
and he sent with her the soldiers Menon had, and Menon himself.
Cyrus with the rest marches through Cappadocia four stages, twenty-five parasangs, to Dana, an inhabited city, large and prosperous.
There they stayed three days;
during this time Cyrus put to death a Persian named Megaphernes, a royal purple-wearer, and another powerful man among his subordinate officials, accusing them of plotting against him.
