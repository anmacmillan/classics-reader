And righteous indignation is a mean between envy and spite, and these concern the pain and pleasure that arise at what happens to our neighbours;
for the man of righteous indignation is pained at those who fare well undeservedly, while the envious man, exceeding him, is pained at all who fare well, and the spiteful man falls so far short of being pained that he even rejoices.
But for these there will be an occasion elsewhere as well;
and as for justice, since it is not spoken of in a single sense, we shall after this distinguish its two kinds and say of each in what way they are means;
and likewise also concerning the rational virtues.
There being, then, three dispositions, two of them vices, the one in the way of excess and the other in the way of deficiency, and one virtue, the mean, all are in a way opposed to all;
for the extremes are contrary both to the middle and to each other, and the middle to the extremes;
for just as the equal is greater in relation to the less but less in relation to the greater, so the mean states exceed in relation to the deficiencies but fall short in relation to the excesses, both in feelings and in actions.
For the brave man appears rash in relation to the coward, but cowardly in relation to the rash man;
and likewise the temperate man appears self-indulgent in relation to the insensible man but insensible in relation to the self-indulgent, and the liberal man prodigal in relation to the illiberal but illiberal in relation to the prodigal.
So the extreme men also push the middle man away, each toward the other, and the coward calls the brave man rash while the rash man calls him cowardly, and analogously in the other cases.
But these being thus opposed to one another, the greatest contrariety is that of the extremes toward each other rather than toward the middle;
for these stand farther apart from one another than from the middle, just as the great stands farther from the small and the small from the great than either does from the equal.
Further, in some extremes a certain likeness to the middle appears, as in rashness toward courage and in prodigality toward liberality;
but between the extremes toward each other there is the greatest unlikeness;
and the things that stand farthest apart from one another are defined as contraries, so that the things standing farther apart are also more contrary.
