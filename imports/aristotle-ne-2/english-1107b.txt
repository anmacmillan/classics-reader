Of those who exceed, the one who exceeds in fearlessness is nameless (for many states are nameless), while the one who exceeds in confidence is rash, and the one who exceeds in fearing and falls short in confidence is a coward.
Concerning pleasures and pains — not all of them, and less so concerning pains — the mean is temperance, the excess self-indulgence.
For people deficient with regard to pleasures hardly occur;
and for that reason such people too have not obtained a name, but let them be called insensible.
Concerning the giving and taking of money, the mean is liberality, and the excess and the deficiency are prodigality and meanness.
But in these they exceed and fall short in contrary ways;
for the prodigal man exceeds in spending and falls short in taking, while the mean man exceeds in taking and falls short in spending.
For now, then, we are speaking in outline and summarily, being content with just this;
later these matters will be determined more exactly.
Concerning money there are also other dispositions: a mean, magnificence (for the magnificent man differs from the liberal man;
for the one is concerned with great things, the other with small), an excess, tastelessness and vulgarity, and a deficiency, niggardliness;
and these differ from the states concerned with liberality, but in what way they differ will be stated later.
Concerning honour and dishonour, the mean is greatness of soul, the excess is what is called a sort of vanity, and the deficiency is pusillanimity;
and as we said liberality stands to magnificence, differing from it by being concerned with small things, so there is also a state that stands thus to greatness of soul, which is concerned with great honour, this state itself being concerned with small honour;
for it is possible to desire honour as one ought, and more than one ought, and less, and the one who exceeds in his desires is called ambitious, the one who falls short unambitious, while the one in the middle is nameless.
The dispositions too are nameless, except the ambition of the ambitious man.
Hence the extremes lay claim to the middle ground;
and we ourselves sometimes call the intermediate man ambitious and sometimes unambitious,
