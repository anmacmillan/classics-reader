And we must consider the things toward which we ourselves are easily carried;
for different men are inclined by nature toward different things;
and this will be knowable from the pleasure and the pain that arise in us.
And we must drag ourselves away toward the contrary;
for by pulling far away from erring we shall come to the middle, which is what those who straighten warped pieces of wood do.
And in everything we must most of all be on guard against the pleasant and pleasure;
for we do not judge it as unbribed judges.
So what the elders of the people felt toward Helen, this we too must feel toward pleasure, and in all things repeat their saying over her;
for by sending it away in this manner we shall err less.
By doing these things, then, to speak in summary, we shall best be able to hit the middle.
But this is perhaps hard, and most of all in particular cases;
for it is not easy to determine both how and with whom and at what sort of things and for how long one should be angry;
for we too sometimes praise those who fall short and call them gentle, and at other times praise those who are harsh, calling them manly.
But the man who deviates a little from doing well is not blamed, whether toward the more or toward the less, only the man who deviates further;
for he does not escape notice.
But up to what point and to what degree a man is blameworthy is not easy to mark off by reasoning;
for neither is anything else among things perceived;
and such things lie among particulars, and the judgment lies in perception.
So much, then, makes this clear: that the middle state is in all things praiseworthy, but that one must incline sometimes toward the excess and sometimes toward the deficiency;
for in this way we shall most easily hit the middle and what is well.
