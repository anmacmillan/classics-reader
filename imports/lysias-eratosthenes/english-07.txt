I went home and told the slave girl to come with me to the market; I took her to the house of one of my friends and told her I had found out everything that was going on in my house.
“So,” I said, “you can choose one of two things: either be whipped and thrown into the mill, and never be free of misery like that again; or tell me the whole truth, suffer no harm, and have my pardon for what you have done wrong.
Don't lie about anything: tell me the whole truth.”
At first she denied it, and told me to do whatever I liked,
since she knew nothing.
But when I mentioned Eratosthenes to her, and said he was the man who had been visiting my wife, she was dumbfounded, thinking I knew everything in detail.
Then she fell at my knees, and after I had promised her that she would come to no harm, she told me everything: first how he had approached her after the funeral, then how in the end she herself had carried his messages, and how my wife had been won over in time, and the ways she let him in, and how at the Thesmophoria, while I was in the country, my wife had gone to the temple with his mother.
Everything else that had happened she told me precisely.
When she had said it all, I told her, “Well then, see that nobody in the world finds out about this;
otherwise nothing you agreed with me will stand.
And I want you to show me this in the act.
I don't need words: I need the deed brought into the open, if it really is so.”
She agreed to do it.
