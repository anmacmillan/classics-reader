After this, gentlemen, some time passed, and I was quite in the dark about my own misfortune, until an old woman came up to me, sent secretly by a woman whom that man was having an affair with, as I learned later.
This woman was angry and felt wronged because he no longer visited her as often, and she kept watch until she found out the reason.
So the old woman, who had been watching for me near my house, came up and said, “Euphiletos, please don't think I have come to you out of mere meddling.
The man who is dishonouring you and your wife happens to be our enemy too.
If you take the slave girl who goes to market and waits on you, and question her under torture, you will learn everything.
The man doing this,” she said, “is Eratosthenes of Oe; he has seduced not only your wife but many others as well.
That is the trade he follows.”
With that, gentlemen, she went away, and I was immediately thrown into turmoil; everything came back to my mind, and I was full of suspicion as I thought how I had been locked in my room, and remembered that on that night the inner door and the street door had creaked, which had never happened before, and that I had thought my wife's face was made up.
All this came into my mind, and I was full of suspicion.
