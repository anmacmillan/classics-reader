When I decided to marry, men of Athens, and brought a wife into my house, for a while I was inclined neither to pester her nor to leave her too free to do as she pleased; I kept an eye on her as far as I could, and paid attention, as was only reasonable.
But when a child was born to me, I began to trust her, and handed over all my affairs to her, thinking this the surest bond between us.
In the early days, men of Athens, she was the best wife there could be:
a clever housekeeper, a good careful manager, running everything with precision.
But when my mother died, her death became the cause of all my troubles.
For my wife went with the funeral procession, was seen there by this man, and in time was seduced.
He watched for the slave girl who went to market, passed messages through her, and ruined my wife.
