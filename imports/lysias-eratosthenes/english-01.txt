I would give a great deal, gentlemen, to have you judge this case as you would judge it if the same thing had happened to you.
For I know very well that if you took the same view of what happens to others as you take of what happens to yourselves, there would not be one of you who was not outraged by what was done; you would all think the penalties too light for men who make a practice of such things.
And this is not how things are judged only among you, but in the whole of Greece:
for this crime alone, in a democracy and an oligarchy alike, the weakest are granted the same redress against the most powerful, so that the lowest man gets the same justice as the highest.
That, gentlemen, is how all mankind regards this outrage: as the most terrible of all.
So on the size of the penalty I believe you are all of one mind, and none of you is so casual as to think that men guilty of such acts deserve pardon, or only a light penalty.
What I think I must prove, gentlemen, is this: that Eratosthenes seduced my wife, corrupted her, shamed my children and outraged me myself by entering my house; that there was no enmity between him and me apart from this; and that I did not do it for money, to make myself rich instead of poor, nor for any other gain except the vengeance the laws allow.
So I will set out my whole story for you from the beginning, leaving nothing out and telling the truth.
For I think my only safety lies in being able to tell you everything that happened.
