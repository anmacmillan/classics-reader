Then Eratosthenes, gentlemen, comes in, and the slave girl wakes me at once and tells me he is inside.
I told her to see to the door, went downstairs quietly and out of the house, and called on one friend after another; some I found at home, others were out of town.
I gathered as many as I could of those who were there and set off.
We got torches from the nearest shop and went in; the door was open, the girl had seen to that.
We pushed open the bedroom door; the first of us in saw him still lying beside my wife, those who came after saw him standing naked on the bed.
I struck him, gentlemen, and knocked him down, pulled his arms behind his back and tied them, and asked him why he was outraging me by entering my house.
He admitted he was in the wrong, but begged and pleaded with me not to kill him and to take money instead.
I replied, “It is not I who will kill you, but the law of the city, which you broke, valuing it less than your pleasures; you chose to commit this crime against my wife and my children rather than obey the laws and behave decently.”
