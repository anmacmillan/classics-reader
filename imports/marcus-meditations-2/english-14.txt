Even if you were going to live three thousand years, and as many times ten thousand, still remember that no one loses any other life than this one he is living, nor lives any other than this one he is losing.
So the longest comes to the same thing as the shortest.
For the present is equal for all, and so what is perishing is equal, and what is being cast away thus shows itself a mere instant.
For no one could cast away either the past or the future;
for what he does not have, how could anyone take this from him?
These two things, then, must be remembered:
one, that all things from everlasting are of the same form and come round in cycles, and it makes no difference whether a man will see the same things in a hundred years or in two hundred or in infinite time;
the other, that both the longest-lived man and he who will die soonest lose the same amount.
For the present is the only thing of which he is going to be deprived, since indeed this alone is what he has, and what a man does not have he does not lose.
