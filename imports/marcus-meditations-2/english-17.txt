Of human life the time is a point, the substance a flux, the perception dim, the composition of the whole body quick to rot, the soul a spinning top, fortune hard to divine, fame uncertain;
to say it in sum, all the things of the body are a river, the things of the soul dream and delusion, life a war and a stranger's sojourn, and after-fame oblivion.
What, then, can escort us on our way?
One thing and one only, philosophy;
and this consists in keeping the divinity within free from violence and unharmed, stronger than pleasures and pains, doing nothing at random nor falsely and with pretence, needing nothing from another's doing or not doing anything;
and further, accepting what happens and is allotted as coming from that same place from which he himself came;
and above all, awaiting death with a gracious mind, as nothing other than a dissolution of the elements out of which each living thing is compounded.
And if there is nothing terrible for the elements themselves in each continually changing into another, why should a man look with suspicion on the change and dissolution of them all?
For it is according to nature;
and nothing is evil that is according to nature.
This at Carnuntum.
