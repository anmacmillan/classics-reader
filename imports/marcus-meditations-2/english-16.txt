The soul of man does violence to itself most of all when it becomes, so far as lies in it, an abscess and as it were a tumour on the universe;
for to be vexed at any of the things that happen is a revolt from nature, in a part of which the natures of each of the other things are contained.
Next, when it turns away from any human being, or is even carried against him as if to do harm, as are the souls of the angry.
Third, it does violence to itself when it is worsted by pleasure or by pain.
Fourth, when it plays a part and does or says anything feignedly and untruthfully.
Fifth, when it lets loose any action or impulse of its own at no target, but does anything whatever at random and without attention, though even the smallest things ought to be done with reference to the end;
and the end of rational creatures is to follow the reason and ordinance of the most venerable city and commonwealth.
