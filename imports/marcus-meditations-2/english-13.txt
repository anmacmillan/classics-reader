Nothing is more wretched than the man who goes round everything in a circle and "searches out the things beneath the earth," as the poet says, and hunts by conjecture for what is in the souls of his neighbours, but does not perceive that it is enough to be with the divinity within himself alone and to serve it genuinely.
And service of it is this: to keep it pure from passion and from rashness and from discontent at what comes from gods and men.
For the things from the gods deserve reverence because of virtue;
and the things from men are dear because of kinship, though sometimes they are also in a way pitiable, because of ignorance of goods and evils;
this maiming is no less than the one that deprives us of the power to distinguish white things and black.
