Whatever mortals commonly say of me (for I am well aware how ill Folly is spoken of, even among the greatest fools), yet that I am the one, the one, I say, who by my divine power gladdens gods and men, there is ample proof in this alone: the moment I came forward to speak before this packed assembly, all your faces suddenly shone with a new and unaccustomed cheerfulness, you so quickly smoothed your brows, you applauded with such glad and friendly laughter, that truly all of you whom I see present on every side seem to me drunk on the nectar of Homer's gods, with a dash of nepenthe besides, whereas before you sat gloomy and anxious, just as if you had lately come back from the cave of Trophonius.
Moreover, just as it usually happens that, as soon as the sun has shown the earth his beautiful golden face, or when after a harsh winter the new spring has breathed on us with gentle west winds, at once a new face, a new colour and plainly a kind of youth returns to all things, so, the moment you caught sight of me, your faces changed.
And so what great orators can otherwise scarcely achieve with a long and long-pondered speech, namely to scatter the troublesome cares of the mind, I have accomplished at once by my mere appearance.
But why I have come forward today in this unusual attire you will hear presently, provided you do not mind lending me your ears as I speak: not, to be sure, the ears you offer to sacred preachers, but those you usually prick up for the quacks of the market-place, for buffoons and jesters, the ears which our famous Midas once displayed to Pan.
For it has pleased me to play the sophist before you for a little while, not indeed of the sort that today drums anxious trifles into schoolboys and teaches them a more than womanish obstinacy in wrangling; no, I shall imitate those ancients who, to avoid the notorious name of 'wise men', preferred to be called sophists.
Their pursuit was to celebrate in encomia the praises of gods and brave men.
An encomium, then, you shall hear, not of Hercules or of Solon, but of my own self, that is, of Folly.
