Why have most people decided to stay two or three days in Lyon? Once I have set out on a journey, I do not rest until I have reached the place I decided on.
On the contrary, I am amazed that anyone can tear himself away from there.
Why on earth?
Because that is the place from which Ulysses' companions could not be torn away: the Sirens are there. Nobody is treated better in his own home than there, at the inn.
What happens?
At table there was always some woman standing by, to cheer up the meal with jokes and charm. And the women there are wonderfully good-looking. First came the lady of the house, who greeted us, told us to be merry, and to take in good part whatever was served.
After her came the daughter, an elegant woman, so cheerful in manner and speech that she could cheer up even Cato himself. And they do not chat as with unknown guests, but as with old acquaintances and close friends.
I recognise the courtesy of the French nation.
But since they could not be there all the time, because they had household duties to see to and the other guests to greet, a certain young girl stood by constantly, equipped for every kind of joke: on her own she was enough to catch everyone's darts; she kept the conversation going until the daughter came back. For the mother was getting on in years.
But what was the fare like, after all? For talk does not fill the belly.
Splendid, truly, so that I wonder how they can entertain guests so cheaply. Again, when the meal is over, they keep a man entertained with witty stories, so that no boredom creeps in. I felt I was at home, not abroad.
What about the bedrooms?
There, everywhere, there were a few girls, laughing, frolicking, playing about: of their own accord they asked if we had any dirty clothes: these they washed, and gave them back washed. In short?
We saw nothing there but girls and women, except in the stable: though even there girls kept bursting in. When you leave they embrace you, and send you off with as much affection as if you were all their brothers or close relations.
