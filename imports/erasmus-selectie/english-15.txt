I would not want you to think it has been made up to show off my wit, as the common run of orators do.
For they, as you know, when they deliver a speech they have laboured over for thirty whole years, and sometimes one that is not even their own, still swear that they wrote it in three days, as if for fun, or even dictated it.
For my part, I have always liked best to say whatever comes onto my tongue, timely or not.
But let nobody now expect me, in the manner of those common rhetoricians, to explain myself by a definition; still less to divide myself into parts.
For both would be ill-omened: either to bound with a limit one whose divine power extends so widely, or to cut up one in whose worship every kind of thing so wholly agrees.
And yet what, after all, is the point of presenting a kind of shadow and image of me by a definition, when you are here in person and see me in person before your very eyes?
For I am, as you see, that true bestower of good things, whom the Latins call Stultitia and the Greeks Moria.
