When there was nothing left at all, one man tore loose a wooden statue of the Virgin Mother, already rotten and hollowed out by mice, and, hugging it tight, began to swim.
Did the lifeboat get through safely?
None died sooner. Besides, thirty men had thrown themselves into it.
By what evil fate did that happen?
Before it could get clear of the big ship, it was capsized by her rolling.
What a wretched business! What then?
While I was looking after others, I nearly perished myself.
How so?
Because there was nothing left that was any use for swimming.
Corks would have been handy there.
At that moment I would rather have had a cheap bit of cork than a golden candlestick.
At last, as I looked round, I thought of the bottom part of the mast: since I could not wrench it free alone, I took on a partner: leaning on it together we committed ourselves to the sea, so that I held the right-hand end and he the left.
While we were being tossed about like this, that priest, the ship's preacher, threw himself right across the middle onto our shoulders. And he was a huge man. We shouted: “Who's that third one? He'll drown the lot of us.” But he answered calmly: “Be of good heart,” he said; “there's room enough; God will be with us.”
Why did he start swimming so late!
Well, he was meant to be in the lifeboat, together with the Dominican: for everyone offered him that honour; but although they had confessed to each other on the ship, they had forgotten some circumstance or other, and so they confessed again there at the ship's side, and each laid his hand on the other; meanwhile the lifeboat went down: for Adam told me all this himself.
What became of the Dominican?
He, so the same man told me, called on the saints for help, threw off his clothes, and trusted himself naked to the swim.
Which saints did he call on?
Dominic, Thomas, Vincent, and some Peter or other, but above all he put his trust in Catherine of Siena.
Did Christ not come into his mind?
So the priest told me.
He would have swum out better if he had not thrown off his holy cowl: once he had taken it off, how could Catherine of Siena recognise him? But go on telling me about yourself.
