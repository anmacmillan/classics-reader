Meanwhile up gets a certain old priest, sixty years of age; his name was Adam. He stripped off his clothes down to his shirt, took off his gaiters and shoes too, and ordered us all to get ready for swimming in the same way.
And so, standing in the middle of the ship, he preached to us from Gerson five truths on the benefit of confession, and urged everyone to prepare himself for both life and death. There was a Dominican on board as well. Those who wished confessed to these two.
And you?
Seeing everything in uproar, I confessed silently to God, condemning my wickedness before him and imploring his mercy.
Where would you have gone, if you had perished like that?
That I left to God as judge. For I had no wish to be my own judge; yet all the while a certain good hope held my mind. While this was going on, a sailor comes back to us in tears.
“Let each man get ready,” he said, “for within a quarter of an hour the ship will be of no use to us.” For by now, torn open in several places, she was taking in the sea.
A little later the sailor tells us that he can see a church tower in the distance, and urges us to implore the help of whatever saint was patron of that church. All fall to their knees and pray to the unknown saint.
If you had called him by name, perhaps he would have heard.
His name was unknown. Meanwhile the captain steers the ship there as best he can, already battered, already taking in water on all sides, and sure to have fallen apart had she not been girded with cables.
A hard situation.
We got so close that the people of the place could see us in danger; and running down in crowds to the water's edge, with their cloaks held up and their hats set on lances, they beckoned us towards them; and flinging their arms towards heaven they showed that they were lamenting our fate.
I'm waiting to hear what happened.
By now the sea had filled the whole ship, so that we would be no safer in the ship than in the sea.
This was the moment to take refuge in the sheet anchor.
Rather in a wretched one. The sailors bail the water out of the lifeboat and let it down into the sea. Everyone tries to throw himself into it, while the sailors shouted amid great uproar that the boat could not hold so large a crowd: each should grab whatever he could and swim.
The situation left no room for slow deliberation: one grabs an oar, another a pole, another a trough, another a bucket, another a plank; and each, relying on his own support, commits himself to the waves.
What happened meanwhile to that poor woman, the only one who didn't wail?
She was the very first to reach the shore.
How could she?
We had set her on a curved plank and tied her on so that she could not easily fall off; we gave her a little board in her hand to use as an oar; and wishing her well we launched her into the waves, pushing her off with a pole to keep her clear of the ship, where the danger was. She held the baby in her left hand and paddled with her right.
What a heroine!
