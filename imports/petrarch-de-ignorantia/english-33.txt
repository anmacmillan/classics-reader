Sometimes, though, causes of disagreement occur that are sharper not only than these but than the earlier ones, kindled not by rivalry in study but by deep hatreds.
For the invectives of Sallust against Cicero and of Aeschines against Demosthenes, and theirs against them, do not arraign minds or styles but characters, and contain something bitter and hostile, indeed nothing peaceable at all.
There is no wit there, no joke, but a contest quite unlike the kind undertaken about letters or for glory; compared with it, all the stings of my judges are games, to be borne with the most level mind, while besides the men you have heard of,
I recall a thousand others contending about letters alone: in particular, on Homer, Aristarchus and Zoilus; on Virgil, Cornificius and Evangelus; on Cicero, Asinius and Calvus; and while I am at it, Gaius comes to mind,
a savage ruler, I admit, but by no means an uneducated one, who considered, as is written, abolishing the poems of Homer, asking why he should not be allowed what Plato had been allowed, who threw Homer out of the city he was founding.
He also came close to removing the writings and portraits of Virgil and of Livy from all the libraries, faulting the one as having no talent and the least learning, the other as wordy and careless in his history.
And Seneca, who pleased people then and pleases them most now, he called sand without lime. And here we are speaking of men, when Leontion, a Greek woman, or rather, as Cicero says, a little courtesan, dared to write against so great a philosopher as Theophrastus.
Who, hearing this, would be indignant at something being said against himself, when such things have been said by such men against such men?
Nothing else remains but for me to pray and beg — I will not say you and a few others, who need no spur to love, but my remaining friends and my censors alike — that from now on they may love me, if not as a lettered man, then as a good one;
and if not even that, then as a friend; and finally, if for want of virtue I do not deserve the name of friend, then at least as one who wishes them well and loves them. THE END.
