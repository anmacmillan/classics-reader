But let me come at last to the matter itself, which you will know as soon as I begin to speak and, unless I am mistaken, knew before I began: as well as I know it myself, and perhaps better, since a friend cares more for a friend's reputation than for his own,
and we grow angry more readily and more creditably at something said against our friends than against ourselves. So many men have despised abuse of themselves, and won praise for it. No one has been able to watch or hear injuries done to a friend and stay calm.
For it is not the same greatness of soul to be unmoved by another's injuries and by one's own.
And how can that be unknown to you which you yourself saw to it that I should know, the thing that grieved you while I shrugged and laughed?
So I shall tell you what you already know, not to make it better known, but so that you may see in what temper I stand against envy, and begin to stand in the same, and not groan over another's wound more heavily than over your own;
and finally so that you may recognise what weapons I use against her, and how, by long practice and hard application, I have gone deaf to the muttering of barkers and grown hard against spiteful teeth.
