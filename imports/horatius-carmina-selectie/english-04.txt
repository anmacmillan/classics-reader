You will live more rightly, Licinius, neither by always
pressing out to the open sea, nor, while you shudder
warily at storms, by hugging too closely
the treacherous shore.
Whoever loves the golden
mean is safe and free from the squalor
of a crumbling house, and, sober, free from a palace
that breeds envy.
More often is the huge pine shaken
by the winds, and lofty towers fall
with a heavier crash, and lightning strikes
the tops of the mountains.
A heart well prepared hopes in adversity
and fears in prosperity for the other
lot. Jupiter brings back ugly winters,
and he likewise
takes them away. If things are bad now, it will not
always be so: sometimes with the lyre
Apollo wakes the silent Muse, and he does not always
stretch his bow.
In hard straits show yourself spirited
and brave: and wisely, likewise,
you will reef, when the wind blows too favourably,
your swelling sails.
