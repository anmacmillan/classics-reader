Or did that most distinguished man, Publius Scipio, the chief priest, as a private citizen kill Tiberius Gracchus, who was only moderately undermining the state, while we, the consuls, shall put up with Catiline, who longs to lay waste the whole world with slaughter and fire?
For I pass over those all too ancient cases, such as that Gaius Servilius Ahala killed with his own hand Spurius Maelius, who was aiming at revolution.
There was, there was once in this republic such courage that brave men punished a dangerous citizen with harsher penalties than the bitterest enemy.
We have a decree of the senate against you, Catiline, forceful and weighty; the state lacks neither counsel nor the authority of this order: it is we, we, I say it openly, we the consuls, who are failing.
The senate once decreed that the consul Lucius Opimius should see to it that the state suffered no harm: not a single night passed: Gaius Gracchus was killed on certain suspicions of sedition, a man whose father, grandfather and ancestors were most distinguished; the ex-consul Marcus Fulvius was killed together with his children.
By a similar decree of the senate the state was entrusted to the consuls Gaius Marius and Lucius Valerius: did death, the penalty of the state, keep the tribune of the plebs Lucius Saturninus and the praetor Gaius Servilius waiting for a single day afterwards?
But we are now for the twentieth day letting the edge of these men's authority grow blunt.
For we have a decree of the senate of this kind, but shut away in the records, as if hidden in a sheath; by that decree, Catiline, you ought to have been put to death at once.
You live, and you live not to lay aside your recklessness but to strengthen it.
I want, senators, to be merciful; I want not to seem lax amid such great dangers to the state; but now I condemn myself for idleness and negligence.
There is a camp in Italy set up against the Roman people in the passes of Etruria; the number of the enemy grows day by day; but the commander of that camp and leader of the enemy you see within the walls, indeed in the senate, plotting every day some ruin for the state from within.
If I now order you, Catiline, to be arrested, if I order you to be killed, I shall have to fear, I suppose, that all good men will rather say this was done too late by me than that anyone will say it was done too cruelly.
But this thing, which ought to have been done long ago, I am for a definite reason not yet brought to do.
You will be killed only then, when no one can be found so wicked, so depraved, so like you, that he does not admit it was done rightly.
As long as there is anyone who dares to defend you, you will live, and you will live as you live now, hemmed in by my many strong guards so that you cannot make a move against the state.
The eyes and ears of many will also watch and guard you without your noticing, as they have done until now.
