Since this is so, Catiline, do you hesitate, if you cannot die with a calm mind, to go away to some other lands and to entrust to flight and solitude that life of yours, snatched from many just and deserved punishments?
'Put the question to the senate,' you say; for that is what you demand, and you say that, if this order decrees that it wishes you to go into exile, you will obey.
I will not put the question, something that is foreign to my character, and yet I will make sure that you understand what these men think of you.
Go out of the city, Catiline, free the state from fear, set out into exile, if you are waiting for this word.
What is this? Do you pay any attention, do you notice at all the silence of these men?
They allow it, they are silent. Why do you wait for the authority of their words, when you see clearly their will while they are silent?
But if I had said this same thing to this excellent young man Publius Sestius, if to that very brave man Marcus Marcellus, the senate would already have laid violent hands on me, the consul, in this very temple, and with the fullest right.
But about you, Catiline, when they keep quiet, they approve; when they allow it, they decree; when they are silent, they shout; and not only these men, whose authority is of course dear to you, though their lives are very cheap, but also those Roman knights, most honourable and excellent men, and the other very brave citizens who stand around the senate, whose crowds you were able to see, whose feelings you were able to perceive, and whose voices you were able to hear a little while ago.
Their hands and weapons I have for a long time now barely been holding back from you; I will easily persuade these same men to escort you all the way to the gates as you leave these things which you have long been eager to lay waste.
