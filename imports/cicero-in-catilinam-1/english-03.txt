For what is there, Catiline, that you can wait for any longer, if neither night can hide your wicked gatherings in darkness nor a private house keep the voices of your conspiracy within its walls, if everything is brought to light, if everything bursts out?
Change that mind of yours now, believe me; forget slaughter and arson.
You are caught on every side; all your plans are clearer to us than daylight, and you may now go over them with me.
Do you remember that I said in the senate on the 21st of October that on a fixed day, which would be the 27th of October, Gaius Manlius, the henchman and agent of your recklessness, would be in arms?
Was I wrong, Catiline, not only about a fact so great, so atrocious and so incredible, but, what is much more to be wondered at, about the day?
I also said in the senate that you had fixed the massacre of the leading men for the 28th of October, at the time when many leading men of the state fled from Rome, not so much to save themselves as to thwart your plans.
Can you deny that on that very day, hemmed in by my guards and my vigilance, you could make no move against the state, when you said that, though the others had left, you were nevertheless content with the slaughter of those of us who had remained?
What? When you were confident that you would seize Praeneste by a night attack on the very 1st of November, did you realise that that colony had been secured on my order by my garrisons, guards and watches?
You do nothing, you plan nothing, you think nothing that I do not only hear but also see and clearly perceive.
Go over with me at last that night before last; you will soon understand that I am far more keenly awake for the safety of the state than you are for its ruin.
I say that on the night before you came into the Scythemakers' Street — I will not speak obscurely — to the house of Marcus Laeca; that many accomplices in the same madness and crime gathered there too.
Do you dare to deny it? Why are you silent? I will prove it, if you deny it.
For I see that there are some here in the senate who were there together with you.
O immortal gods! Where in the world are we? What kind of state do we have?
In what city are we living? Here, here in our own number, senators, in this most sacred and most weighty council in the world, are men who are plotting the death of us all, the destruction of this city and indeed of the whole world.
These men I, the consul, see and ask for their opinion on affairs of state, and men who ought to have been butchered with the sword I do not yet wound even with my voice!
So you were at Laeca's house that night, Catiline; you divided up the regions of Italy, you decided where you wanted each man to go, you chose whom to leave in Rome and whom to take with you, you marked out the parts of the city for burning, you confirmed that you yourself would soon be leaving, you said that even now there was a small delay for you, because I was alive.
Two Roman knights were found to free you from that concern and to promise that that very night, a little before dawn, they would kill me in my bed.
All this I found out almost before your meeting had broken up; I secured and strengthened my house with larger guards, I shut out the men you had sent to greet me in the morning, since the very men had come who I had already predicted to many eminent men would come to me at that hour.
