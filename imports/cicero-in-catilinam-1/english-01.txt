How long, Catiline, will you go on abusing our patience?
How long will that madness of yours still make fools of us? To what limit will your unbridled recklessness keep flaunting itself?
Has nothing moved you, not the night garrison on the Palatine, not the watches of the city, not the fear of the people, not the gathering of all good men, not this most strongly guarded place for holding the senate, not the faces and expressions of these men here?
Do you not realise that your plans are exposed, do you not see that your conspiracy is already held in check, bound tight by the knowledge of all these men?
What you did last night, what the night before, where you were, whom you called together, what plan you adopted: which of us do you think is unaware of it?
What times, what morals! The senate understands these things, the consul sees them; yet this man lives.
Lives? No, more than that: he even comes into the senate, he takes part in the state's deliberations, he marks and singles out with his eyes each one of us for slaughter.
And we, brave men that we are, think we are doing enough for the state if we avoid that man's madness and weapons.
You ought long ago, Catiline, to have been led to your death on the consul's order; the ruin which you have long been plotting against us all ought to have been brought down on you.
