Since this is so, Catiline, carry on where you have begun: leave the city at last; the gates are open; set out.
That Manlian camp of yours has been missing you as its commander for far too long.
Take all your men with you too, or if not all, as many as possible; purge the city.
You will free me from a great fear, provided only that there is a wall between me and you.
You can no longer stay among us; I will not bear it, I will not endure it, I will not allow it.
Great thanks must be given to the immortal gods, and to this very Jupiter Stator, the most ancient guardian of this city, because we have so often now escaped this plague, so foul, so horrible and so deadly to the state.
The supreme safety of the state must not be put at risk too often because of one man.
As long as you plotted against me when I was consul-elect, Catiline, I defended myself not with a public guard but by my own private vigilance.
When at the last consular elections you wanted to kill me, the consul, on the Campus Martius, together with your rivals, I crushed your wicked attempts with the protection and forces of my friends, without raising any public alarm; in short, whenever you went for me, I opposed you by my own efforts, although I saw that my ruin would be bound up with a great disaster for the state.
Now you openly attack the whole state: the temples of the immortal gods, the houses of the city, the life of all citizens, the whole of Italy you summon to destruction and devastation.
Therefore, since I do not yet dare to do what comes first and what befits this command and the discipline of our ancestors, I shall do what is milder in severity and more useful for the common safety.
For if I order you to be killed, the rest of the conspirators' band will remain in the state; but if you leave, as I have long been urging you to, the great and deadly bilge-water of the state, your companions, will be drained out of the city.
What is it, Catiline?
