The man of Abydos sends the greeting he would rather carry himself,
if the wave of the sea should fall, to you, girl of Sestos.
If the gods favour me, if they support lovers,
you will read these words of mine with reluctant eyes.
But they do not favour me; for why do they delay my wishes
and not allow me to race through the familiar water?
You yourself see the sky, blacker than pitch, and the straits
churned by winds and scarcely passable even for hollow ships.
One sailor—and a bold one—set out from the harbour;
through him this letter of mine is delivered to you.
I was about to board, except that, as he loosened the bow’s ropes,
all Abydos was watching from the lookout points.
I could not hide from my parents as I had before,
and the love we want concealed would not have remained hidden.
At once, as I wrote this, I said, “Happy letter, go!
Soon she will stretch out her beautiful hand to you.
Perhaps she will even touch you with her lips as she brings them near,
while she tries to break the fastening with her snow-white tooth.”
After I had spoken such words in a low murmur,
my right hand said the rest upon the paper.
But how much rather would I have it swim than write,
and diligently carry me through the accustomed waters!
That hand is indeed better suited to strike the calm sea,
yet it is also a fitting servant of my feelings.
