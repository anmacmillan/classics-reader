Night was just beginning—for remembering it is a pleasure—
when, a lover, I went out through my father’s doors.
Without delay, laying aside fear together with my clothes,
I cast my supple arms into the liquid sea.
The moon offered a trembling light to me as I went,
like a helpful companion upon my path.
Looking up at her, I said, “Favour me, bright goddess,
and let the rocks of Latmos enter your mind!
Endymion does not allow you to have a hard heart.
Turn your face, I pray, towards my secret love!
You, a goddess, slipped down from heaven to seek a mortal;
let me speak the truth—the girl I follow is herself a goddess.
Not to mention her character, worthy of a heavenly heart,
such beauty belongs only among true goddesses.
No face ranks before Venus’s face and yours;
do not trust my words—look for yourself!
As, when you shine with pure silver rays,
all the stars yield to your flames,
so she is more beautiful than all beautiful women.
If you doubt it, Cynthia, your light is blind.”
Having said this, or certainly words not unlike these,
I was carried through waters that almost yielded of their own accord.
The wave shone with the image of the reflected moon,
and there was daylight splendour in the silent night;
no voice anywhere, no sound reached my ears
except the murmur of water displaced by my body.
Only the kingfishers, remembering beloved Ceyx,
seemed to me to murmur some sweet lament.
