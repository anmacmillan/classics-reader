Of course you will be distressed by the omen of my death,
and my letter will be hateful to you in this part.
I stop—do not complain! But so that the sea may end its anger,
please make your prayers join mine.
We need a brief calm while I cross over there;
when I have touched your shore, let winter persist!
There is a suitable dock there for my little vessel,
and my ship rests better in no other water.
Let Boreas shut me in where it is sweet to remain!
Then I shall be slow to swim, then I shall be cautious,
nor shall I hurl any insults at the deaf waves,
nor complain that the straits are harsh to one about to swim.
Let winds and tender arms hold me there together,
and let me be hindered there by those two causes!
When winter permits, I shall use my body’s oars;
only keep the light always within sight!
Meanwhile let my letter spend the night with you in my place;
I pray that I myself may follow it with the least delay!
