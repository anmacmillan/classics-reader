Willem, who made many books,
over which he often lay awake,
was so greatly troubled
that the adventure of Reynaert
had remained unmade in Dutch
– which Willem has not fully written –
that he sought out the life of Reynaert
and, following the French books,
has thus begun it in Dutch.
May God grant us His help.
Now my mind turns to this:
that I ask, at this beginning,
both the churls and the fools,
should they come to where they hear
these rhymes and these words,
which seem useless to them to hear,
to leave them uncriticized.
They resemble the raven too much,
who is always just as malsch (foolish — a disputed word; see note).
They call such rhymes false
of which they know no more
than I do of what they're called,
those who now live in Babylon.
If they did well, they would give it up.
I do not say this for my own sake:
my writing would have fallen silent,
had not one lady asked me,
who in great courtliness
gladly conducts her affairs.
She asked me that I should make
this adventure of Reynaert.
Though I find fault with the grumblers
and the churls and the fools,
I want those to hear it
who gladly practise honour
and turn their minds to it,
so that they live courteously,
be they poor, be they rich,
who understand it with good sense.
Now hear how I begin here!
