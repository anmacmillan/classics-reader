'Whenever I consider the causes of the war and the necessity we are under, I have great confidence that this day and your unity will be the beginning of freedom for the whole of Britain: for you have all come together, and you have never known slavery; beyond us there are no more lands, and not even the sea is safe, with the Roman fleet bearing down on us.
So battle and arms, which bring honour to the brave, are also the safest course even for cowards.
Earlier battles, fought against the Romans with varying fortune, had their hope and reserve in our hands, because we, the noblest people of all Britain and for that reason settled in its innermost sanctuary, looking out on no shores of enslaved peoples, kept even our eyes untouched by the contact of tyranny.
We, the last people on earth and the last of the free, have been protected until this day by our very remoteness and the obscurity of our name: now the furthest end of Britain lies open, and everything unknown is taken for something magnificent; but there is no longer any people beyond us, nothing but waves and rocks, and the Romans, more dangerous still, whose arrogance you would try in vain to escape by submission and restraint.
Plunderers of the world, now that the lands have run out for them as they lay everything waste, they ransack the sea: greedy if the enemy is rich, ambitious if he is poor; men whom neither East nor West has glutted: alone of all mankind they covet wealth and poverty with equal passion.
To steal, to slaughter, to plunder, they call by the false name of empire, and where they make a desert, they call it peace.
