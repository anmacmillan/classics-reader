After this Lucius Arruntius, in a speech not very different from Gallus', gave just as much offence, although Tiberius had no long-standing anger against Arruntius: but he distrusted him because he was rich, energetic, of outstanding abilities and with a public reputation to match.
For Augustus, in his last conversations, when discussing who would be capable of taking the first place but would refuse it, who were unequal to it but wanted it, and who were both able and eager, had said that Manius Lepidus was capable but disdainful, Asinius Gallus eager but not up to it, and Lucius Arruntius not unworthy and, if the chance arose, likely to dare.
About the first two there is agreement; instead of Arruntius some have recorded Gnaeus Piso; and all of them except Lepidus were soon brought down on various charges engineered by Tiberius.
Quintus Haterius and Mamercus Scaurus also grated on his suspicious mind, Haterius when he had said: 'How long, Caesar, will you allow the state to be without a head?'
Scaurus because he had said that there was hope that the senate's prayers would not be in vain, since he had not used his tribunician power to veto the consuls' motion.
He immediately attacked Haterius; Scaurus, against whom his anger was more implacable, he passed over in silence.
Worn out by the clamour of all and the protests of individuals, he gradually gave way, not so as to admit that he was taking on the rule, but so as to stop refusing and being begged.
It is well established that Haterius, when he had entered the Palace to beg forgiveness and threw himself at the knees of Tiberius as he walked, was nearly killed by the soldiers, because Tiberius fell forward, whether by chance or tripped up by Haterius' hands.
Yet he was not softened by the danger to such a man, until Haterius begged Augusta and was protected by her most earnest pleas.
