'Nature willed that each man's children and kin should be dearest to him: these are carried off by levies to be slaves elsewhere; our wives and sisters, even if they have escaped the lust of an enemy, are defiled by men calling themselves friends and guests.
Our goods and fortunes are ground down into tribute, our land and harvest into grain requisitions, our very bodies and hands into clearing forests and marshes, amid blows and insults.
Slaves born to slavery are sold once and for all, and are even fed by their masters: Britain buys her own slavery every day, and every day feeds it.
And just as in a household the newest slave is a laughing-stock even to his fellow slaves, so in this long-established servitude of the whole world we, the newcomers and worthless, are marked out for destruction; for we have no fields or mines or harbours for whose working we might be kept alive.
Moreover, courage and spirit in subjects are unwelcome to rulers; and our very remoteness and seclusion, the safer they make us, the more suspicion they arouse.
So, now that all hope of mercy is gone, take courage at last, both you who hold life dearest and you who hold glory dearest.
The Brigantes, with a woman as leader, were able to burn a colony, storm a camp, and, had success not turned into carelessness, throw off the yoke: we, untouched and unconquered, who will fight for freedom and not for regret, let us show at the very first clash what kind of men Caledonia has kept in reserve for herself.
