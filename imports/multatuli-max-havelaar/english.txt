I am a coffee broker, and live on the Lauriergracht, No. 37. It is not my habit to write novels, or such things, and it took a long time before I got around to ordering an extra couple of reams of paper and beginning the work that you, dear reader, have just now taken in hand, and which you must read whether you are a coffee broker or something else. Not only did I never write anything resembling a novel, I don't even like reading such things, because I am a man of business. For years I have asked myself what use such things serve, and I am astonished at the shamelessness with which a poet or novelist dares to pin something on you that never happened, and mostly cannot happen. If I, in my own trade—I am a coffee broker, and live on the Lauriergracht No. 37—gave a principal—a principal is someone who sells coffee—a report in which even a small fraction of the untruths occurred that make up the main substance of poems and novels, he would immediately go to Busselinck & Waterman. They are coffee brokers too, though you needn't know their address. So I take good care not to write novels, or make other false reports. I have also always noticed that people who get mixed up in such things usually come off badly. I am forty-three years old, have attended the Exchange for twenty years, and can therefore step forward when someone is called for who has experience. I have seen quite a few houses fall! And usually, when I looked into the causes, it seemed to me that they had to be sought in the wrong direction that most people were given in their youth.
