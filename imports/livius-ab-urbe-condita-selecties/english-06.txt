At the first outbreak of the disturbance Numitor kept declaring that enemies had broken into the city and attacked the palace, and drew off the fighting men of Alba to the citadel to hold it with a garrison and arms; then, when he saw the young men, the killing done, coming towards him to congratulate him, he at once called an assembly and set out his brother's crimes against him, the origin of his grandsons, how they had been born, how raised, how recognised, and then the killing of the tyrant, and that he himself was responsible for it.
The young men marched in a body through the middle of the assembly and hailed their grandfather as king, and the unanimous shout that followed from the whole crowd confirmed the title and power of the king.
So, when Alba had been entrusted to Numitor, Romulus and Remus were seized by the desire to found a city in the place where they had been exposed and where they had been brought up.
There was also a surplus of Albans and Latins; besides this, shepherds had joined them too, and all these together easily made them hope that Alba would be small, and Lavinium small, compared with the city that was to be founded.
Then into these plans came the ancestral curse, the desire for kingship, and from it arose a shameful contest out of a quite peaceful beginning.
Since they were twins and respect for age could not decide between them, in order that the gods under whose protection these places lay might choose by augury who should give his name to the new city and who should rule it once founded, Romulus took the Palatine and Remus the Aventine as their sacred areas for taking the auspices.
