They say that even then this festival of the Lupercal was held on the Palatine, and that the hill was called Pallantium after Pallanteum, a city in Arcadia, and later Palatium; that there Evander, who belonged to that Arcadian race and had held the region many ages before, established a rite brought from Arcadia, in which naked young men ran about in sport and wantonness in honour of Lycaean Pan, whom the Romans later called Inuus.
While they were given up to this festival, since the ritual was well known, robbers angry at the loss of their booty lay in wait for them, and, though Romulus defended himself by force, they captured Remus, and handed their prisoner over to King Amulius, even going so far as to accuse him themselves.
The main charge they brought was that the brothers made raids on Numitor's lands; that from there, having gathered a band of young men, they drove off plunder like enemies.
So Remus is handed over to Numitor for punishment.
From the very beginning Faustulus had hoped that it was a royal child being brought up in his house; for he knew both that infants had been exposed on the king's orders and that the time when he himself had taken them up matched exactly; but he had not wanted the matter to be revealed before it was ripe, except either at a good opportunity or under necessity.
Necessity came first: so, driven by fear, he reveals the matter to Romulus.
By chance Numitor too, while he held Remus in custody and had heard that the brothers were twins, had been touched by the memory of his grandsons, comparing both their age and their nature, which was anything but servile; and by questioning he arrived at the same point, so that he came close to recognising Remus.
So on every side a trap is woven around the king.
Romulus did not go with a body of young men (for he was no match for open force), but ordered the shepherds to come to the palace at a set time, each by a different route, and so makes his attack on the king; and from Numitor's house Remus helps with another band he had got together.
So he cuts the king down.
