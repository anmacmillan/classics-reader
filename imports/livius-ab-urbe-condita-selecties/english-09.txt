A few days later Sextus Tarquinius, without Collatinus’ knowledge, came to Collatia with a single companion.
There he was warmly received by people who knew nothing of his purpose, and after dinner he was shown to the guest-chamber; burning with passion, once everything around seemed safe enough and everyone asleep, he drew his sword, came to Lucretia as she slept, and pressing the woman’s breast down with his left hand, “Be silent, Lucretia,” he said; “I am Sextus Tarquinius; I have a sword in my hand; you will die if you utter a sound.”
When the woman, startled out of sleep, saw no help anywhere and death hanging close over her, then Tarquin began to confess his love, to plead, to mix threats with prayers, to turn a woman’s heart every way he could.
When he saw that she was resolute and not to be moved even by fear of death, he added disgrace to fear: he said that he would lay a naked slave with his throat cut beside her dead body, so that she would be said to have been killed in a sordid adultery.
When by this terror his lust had overcome her steadfast chastity as if by force, and Tarquin had gone away from there exulting in his conquest of a woman’s honour, Lucretia, grief-stricken at so great an evil, sent the same message to her father in Rome and to her husband at Ardea, that they should come, each with one trusted friend; that this must be done, and done quickly; that a dreadful thing had happened.
