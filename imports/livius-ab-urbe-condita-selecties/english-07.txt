Remus, it is said, received an omen first, six vultures; and when the omen had just been announced, twice the number showed themselves to Romulus, and each had been hailed as king by his own followers: the one side claimed the kingship on the ground of priority in time, the other on the number of the birds.
Then they met in an exchange of words, and from angry wrangling they turned to bloodshed; there, in the brawl, Remus was struck and fell.
The more common story is that Remus, to mock his brother, leapt over the new walls, and was then killed by the enraged Romulus, who added these taunting words as well: “So perish from now on whoever else leaps over my walls.”
In this way Romulus alone gained power; the city, once founded, was called after its founder's name.
