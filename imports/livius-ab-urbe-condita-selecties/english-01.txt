Whether I shall be doing something worth the effort if I write out in full the history of the Roman people from the very beginnings of the city, I do not really know, nor, if I knew, would I dare to say so, since I see that this is an old and a well-worn undertaking, while new writers keep believing that they will either bring something more certain to the facts or surpass the rough manner of antiquity by their art of writing.
However that may turn out, it will still be a pleasure to have done my own part, as far as a man can, for the memory of the deeds of the foremost people on earth; and if, in so great a crowd of writers, my own fame should remain in obscurity, I would console myself with the renown and greatness of those who will overshadow my name.
Besides, the subject demands immense labour, since it reaches back more than seven hundred years and, having set out from small beginnings, has grown so large that it now struggles under its own greatness; and I have no doubt that for most readers the earliest origins and the times just after them will give less pleasure, as they hurry on to these recent events in which the strength of a people long supreme is destroying itself:
I, on the contrary, shall seek this reward too for my labour, that I may turn away from the sight of the evils which our age has witnessed for so many years, at least for as long as I am recalling those ancient times with all my mind, free of every care which, though it could not turn a writer's mind from the truth, might still make it anxious.
