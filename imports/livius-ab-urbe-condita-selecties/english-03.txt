Next Proca reigns.
He fathers Numitor and Amulius; to Numitor, who was the elder son, he bequeaths the ancient kingdom of the Silvian house.
Yet force prevailed over the father's wishes and respect for seniority: Amulius drives out his brother and reigns.
He adds crime to crime: he kills his brother's male offspring, and he robs his brother's daughter, Rea Silvia, of the hope of children under the pretence of an honour, since he had chosen her as a Vestal and so bound her to lifelong virginity.
But the origin of so great a city and the beginning of the mightiest empire after the power of the gods were owed, I believe, to the Fates.
The Vestal was raped and gave birth to twins, and, whether she truly believed it or because a god was a more honourable author of her fault, she names Mars as the father of her doubtful offspring.
But neither gods nor men protect either her or her children from the king's cruelty: the priestess is bound and put in prison, and he orders the boys to be thrown into the flowing river.
