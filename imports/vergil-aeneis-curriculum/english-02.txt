Then indeed a new fear worms its way through the trembling hearts
of all, and they say that he has deservedly paid for his crime,
Laocoön, who wounded the sacred oak with his spear-point
and hurled his wicked spear into its flank.
"The image must be brought to its temple, the goddess's power must be entreated,"
they all shout.
We breach the walls and lay open the city's defences.
All set themselves to the work, and under its feet they slip
rolling wheels, and around its neck hempen ropes
they stretch: the machine of doom climbs the walls,
pregnant with arms. Boys and unmarried girls around it
sing sacred hymns and are glad to touch the rope with their hands.
On it comes, and glides menacingly into the heart of the city.
O fatherland, O Ilium, home of gods, and walls, famed in war,
of the Dardanians! Four times, right on the threshold of the gate,
it stopped, and four times the arms clanged inside its belly:
yet we press on, heedless and blind with frenzy,
and set the ill-starred monster on the sacred citadel.
Then too Cassandra opens her lips to foretell the coming doom,
lips that by the god's command were never believed by the Trojans.
We poor wretches, for whom that day was to be the last,
deck the shrines of the gods with festive foliage throughout the city.
