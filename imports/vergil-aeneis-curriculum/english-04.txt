She had spoken. He, because of Jupiter's warnings, kept his eyes
unmoved, and with an effort he pressed down the pain beneath his heart.
At last he answers briefly: "I will never deny, queen, that you
have earned the very many things you can list in speaking;
nor will I be sorry to remember Elissa
as long as I remember myself, as long as breath rules these limbs.
I will say a few words to the point. I did not hope to hide this departure
by stealth (do not imagine it), nor did I ever hold out a husband's
wedding torches or enter into such a pact.
If the fates allowed me to lead my life
under my own direction and to settle my cares as I myself wished,
I would first care for the city of Troy and the dear remains
of my people; Priam's high halls would still stand,
and with my own hand I would have built a new Pergamum for the defeated.
But now Grynean Apollo has ordered me to make for great Italy,
to Italy the oracles of Lycia have ordered me:
this is my love, this is my homeland. If the citadels of Carthage
and the sight of a Libyan city hold you, a Phoenician woman,
what grudge is it, then, that the Trojans settle in the Ausonian land?
We too have the right to seek foreign kingdoms.
My father Anchises, as often as night covers the earth with damp shadows,
as often as the fiery stars rise,
warns me in my dreams, and his troubled ghost frightens me;
so does the boy Ascanius, and the wrong done to his dear head,
whom I am cheating of the kingdom of Hesperia and the fated fields.
Now even the messenger of the gods, sent by Jupiter himself
(I swear by both our heads), has brought his orders down through the swift air;
I myself saw the god in clear daylight
entering the walls, and I drank in his voice with these ears.
Stop inflaming both me and yourself with your complaints:
I do not go to Italy of my own free will."
