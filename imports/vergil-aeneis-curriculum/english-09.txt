Meanwhile Aeneas sees, in a secluded valley,
a remote grove and thickets rustling in the woods,
and the river Lethe, which flows past those peaceful homes.
Around it countless races and peoples were flitting;
and, as in meadows when bees on a clear summer day
settle on flowers of every kind, and round the white
lilies they swarm, the whole plain hums with their murmuring.
He shudders at the sudden sight and asks the reasons,
Aeneas, not understanding: what is that river further on,
and who are the men that fill its banks in such a great throng?
Then father Anchises said: "The souls to whom, by fate, second
bodies are owed, at the water of the river Lethe
drink the waters that free from care, and long forgetfulness.
These, indeed, I want to tell you of and show you face to face;
I have long wanted to list for you this offspring of my line,
so that you may rejoice with me all the more at finding Italy."
"O father, must we really believe that some go up from here to the sky,
souls rising on high, and return once more to sluggish
bodies? What is this dreadful longing of the poor souls for the light?"
"I will tell you indeed, my son, and not keep you in suspense,"
Anchises replies, and he sets out each thing in order.
