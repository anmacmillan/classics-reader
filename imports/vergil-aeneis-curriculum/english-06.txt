But Dido, trembling and driven wild by her monstrous plan,
rolling her bloodshot eyes, her quivering
cheeks flecked with spots, and pale with the death to come,
bursts into the inner courtyard of the palace, and the high
pyre she climbs in a frenzy, and unsheathes the sword
of the Trojan, a gift not asked for such a purpose.
Here, after she caught sight of the Trojan clothes and the familiar bed,
and lingered a little in tears and thought,
she lay down on the couch and spoke her last words:
"Sweet relics, sweet while the fates and the god allowed,
receive this soul and free me from these cares.
I have lived, and I have run the course that fortune gave me,
and now my great shade will go beneath the earth.
I founded a glorious city; I saw my own walls rise;
I avenged my husband and made my hostile brother pay;
happy, alas, too happy, if only
the Trojan ships had never touched our shores!"
She spoke, and pressing her face into the couch: "I shall die unavenged,
but let me die," she said. "Thus, thus I am glad to go beneath the shades.
Let him drink in this fire with his eyes from the deep sea, the cruel
Trojan, and carry with him the omens of my death."
She had spoken; and in the midst of these words her attendants see her
collapsed on the sword, and the sword foaming
with blood, and her hands spattered. A cry rises to the high
halls; Rumour runs wild through the shaken city.
With laments and groans and the wailing of women
the houses roar; the sky echoes with loud cries of mourning,
just as if, with the enemy let in, all
Carthage or ancient Tyre were falling, and raging flames
were rolling over the roofs of men and over those of the gods.
