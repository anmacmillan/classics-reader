Here another, greater thing, and far more fearful, is thrust
before us wretches, and throws our unsuspecting hearts into turmoil.
Laocoön, chosen by lot as priest of Neptune,
was sacrificing a huge bull at the customary altars.
But look: from Tenedos, across the calm deep, twin —
I shudder as I tell it — serpents with enormous coils
lie heavy on the sea and side by side make for the shore;
their breasts, raised up among the waves, and their crests,
blood-red, rise above the waves; the rest of them skims the sea
behind, and curves their huge backs in winding coils.
A roar goes up as the salt sea foams; and now they were reaching the fields,
their blazing eyes shot through with blood and fire,
and they licked their hissing mouths with flickering tongues.
We scatter, pale at the sight; they, in a steady line,
make for Laocoön; and first each serpent seizes the small
bodies of his two sons, wrapping itself around them,
entwines them, and feeds with its bite on their pitiful limbs;
then they seize the man himself as he comes to help, carrying weapons,
and bind him in huge coils; and now,
twice wound round his middle, twice winding their scaly
backs round his neck, they tower over him with heads and lofty necks.
At once he strains with his hands to tear the knots apart,
his priestly headbands drenched with gore and black venom,
and at once he raises horrifying cries to the stars:
like the bellowing when a wounded bull flees from the altar
and has shaken the badly aimed axe from its neck.
But the twin serpents glide away to the high shrine
and escape, and make for the citadel of fierce Tritonian Minerva,
and hide beneath the goddess's feet, under the round of her shield.
