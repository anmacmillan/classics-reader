Now turn both your eyes this way, look at this people,
your own Romans. Here is Caesar, and all the descendants of Iulus
who are destined to come beneath the great vault of heaven.
This is the man, this is he, whom you so often hear promised to you,
Augustus Caesar, son of a god, who will found again the golden
age in Latium, through the fields once ruled
by Saturn; beyond both the Garamantes and the Indians
he will extend the empire: there lies a land beyond the stars,
beyond the paths of the year and the sun, where sky-bearing Atlas
turns on his shoulder the heavens, studded with blazing stars.
Even now, at the prospect of his coming, both the Caspian kingdoms
shudder at the oracles of the gods, and the Maeotic land,
and the mouths of the sevenfold Nile are thrown into alarm.
Not even Hercules travelled over so much of the earth,
though he shot the bronze-footed deer, or brought peace to the woods
of Erymanthus, and made Lerna tremble at his bow;
nor he who, victorious, steers his team with reins of vine leaves,
Bacchus, driving his tigers down from the lofty peak of Nysa.
And do we still hesitate to extend our strength by deeds of courage,
or does fear prevent us from settling in the land of Ausonia?
