But father Anchises, deep in a green valley,
the souls shut in there, who were to go up to the light above,
was surveying with eager attention, thinking them over; and the whole number of his own people
he happened to be reviewing, and his dear descendants,
the fates and fortunes of those men, their characters and their deeds.
And when he sees, coming towards him across the grass,
Aeneas, he eagerly stretched out both hands,
tears poured down his cheeks, and these words fell from his lips:
"Have you come at last? Has your devotion, so long awaited by your father,
overcome the hard journey? Am I allowed to look on your face,
my son, and to hear familiar voices and answer them?
That is indeed how I reckoned it in my mind, and I believed it would come,
counting the days, and my anxious hope has not deceived me.
Over what lands, across what great seas have you travelled
for me to welcome you! Tossed by what great dangers, my son!
How I feared that the kingdom of Libya might do you some harm!"
But he said: "Your image, father, your sad image,
appearing to me again and again, drove me to make for these thresholds:
my fleet lies at anchor in the Tyrrhenian sea. Let me clasp your right hand,
let me, father, and do not draw away from my embrace."
As he spoke, he wet his face at the same time with a flood of tears.
Three times he tried there to put his arms around his neck,
three times the image, clasped in vain, slipped out of his hands,
like the light winds and most like a winged dream.
