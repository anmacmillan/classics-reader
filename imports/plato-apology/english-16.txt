Now if by saying this I corrupt the young, then this would be harmful;
but if anyone says that I say anything other than this, he is talking nonsense.
In view of this, I would say, men of Athens, either obey Anytus or don't, and either acquit me or don't, knowing that I would not act otherwise, not even if I were to die many times over.
Don't make an uproar, men of Athens, but keep to what I asked of you, not to make an uproar at what I say but to listen;
for, as I think, you will benefit from listening.
For I am going to tell you some other things at which you will perhaps shout out;
but don't do that at all.
For be sure of this: if you kill me, being the kind of man I say I am, you will not harm me more than yourselves;
for neither Meletus nor Anytus could harm me at all — he would not even be able to — for I do not think it is permitted for a better man to be harmed by a worse.
He might, perhaps, kill me or banish me or strip me of my rights;
but while he, perhaps, and others too, think these are great evils, I do not; I think it is far worse to do what this man is doing now, trying to kill a man unjustly.
So now, men of Athens, far from making my defence for my own sake, as one might think, I am making it for yours, so that you do not do wrong in the matter of the god's gift to you by condemning me.
For if you kill me, you will not easily find another like me, simply — even if it sounds rather comic to say — attached to the city by the god as to a large and well-bred horse, a bit sluggish because of its size and needing to be woken up by some gadfly; just so, it seems to me, the god has attached me to the city as someone of that kind, who never stops waking you up and persuading and reproaching each one of you, settling on you everywhere all day long.
So another like me will not easily come to you, gentlemen; but if you take my advice, you will spare me;
but perhaps you, annoyed like people woken up while dozing, would swat me and, listening to Anytus, would lightly kill me, and then you would spend the rest of your lives asleep, unless the god, caring for you, were to send you someone else.
