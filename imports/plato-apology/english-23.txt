For if I say that this would be disobeying the god and that for that reason I cannot keep quiet, you will not believe me, thinking I am being ironic;
and if again I say that this is in fact the greatest good for a human being, to discuss virtue every day, and the other things you hear me talking about as I examine myself and others, and that the unexamined life is not worth living for a human being, you will believe me even less when I say it.
But things are as I say, gentlemen, though it is not easy to convince you.
And at the same time I am not used to thinking myself deserving of anything bad.
For if I had money, I would have proposed a fine of as much as I could pay, for that would have done me no harm;
but as it is I have none, unless you are willing to set my penalty at as much as I could pay.
Perhaps I could pay you a mina of silver;
So that is the penalty I propose.
But Plato here, men of Athens, and Crito and Critobulus and Apollodorus are urging me to propose thirty minae, and they will stand surety for it themselves;
So I propose that amount, and these men will be reliable guarantors of the money to you.
