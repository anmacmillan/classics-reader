And what about this?
Do the listeners here make them better or not?
They do too.
What about the members of the Council?
The members of the Council too.
But then, Meletus, surely those in the Assembly, the assemblymen, don't corrupt the young?
Or do they all make them better too?
They too.
So all the Athenians, it seems, make them fine and good except me, and I alone corrupt them.
Is that what you're saying?
That is exactly what I'm saying.
You have found me guilty of great bad luck!
And answer me this:
Do you think it is like this with horses too?
That all people make them better, and only one corrupts them?
Or quite the opposite: that one person, or very few, the horse-trainers, can make them better, while most people, if they have to do with horses and use them, spoil them?
Isn't that so, Meletus, with horses and with all other animals?
Of course it is, whether you and Anytus deny it or not;
for the young would be lucky indeed if only one person corrupted them and everyone else did them good.
But no, Meletus: you show clearly enough that you have never given a thought to the young, and you plainly reveal your own indifference: you have never cared about the things you bring me to court for.
