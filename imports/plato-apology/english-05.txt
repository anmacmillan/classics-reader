Listen, then.
And perhaps some of you will think I am joking;
but be well assured, I will tell you the whole truth.
For I, men of Athens, have got this name through nothing other than a certain wisdom.
What kind of wisdom is that?
The kind that is perhaps human wisdom;
for in reality I may well be wise in that.
But these men I mentioned just now may perhaps be wise with a wisdom greater than human, or else I do not know what to say;
for I certainly do not possess it myself, and whoever says I do is lying and speaks to slander me.
And please, men of Athens, do not make an uproar, not even if I seem to you to be saying something boastful;
for the words I shall speak are not my own, but I will refer you to a speaker who is worthy of your trust.
For of my wisdom, if it is wisdom at all and of what kind, I will offer you as witness the god at Delphi.
You know Chaerephon, I suppose.
He was my friend from youth, and a friend of your democratic party too, and he shared in the recent exile and came back with you.
And you know what sort of man Chaerephon was, how impetuous in whatever he set out to do.
Well, once he went to Delphi and dared to ask the oracle this— and, as I said, do not make an uproar, gentlemen— he asked whether there was anyone wiser than me.
The Pythia replied that no one was wiser.
And about this his brother here will testify to you, since Chaerephon himself has died.
Now consider why I am telling you this;
for I am going to explain to you where the slander against me has come from.
