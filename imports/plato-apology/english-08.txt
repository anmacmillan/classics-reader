From this examination, men of Athens, many enmities have arisen against me, of the harshest and most oppressive kind, so that many slanders have come from them, and I have been given this name: that I am wise.
For each time, those present think that I myself am wise in the things in which I refute someone else.
But in reality, gentlemen, it is probably the god who is wise, and in this oracle he is saying this: that human wisdom is worth little or nothing.
And he appears to mention this Socrates, and to have made use of my name, taking me as an example, as if he were to say: 'That one among you, mortals, is wisest who, like Socrates, has recognised that in truth he is worth nothing where wisdom is concerned.'
So even now I still go around, searching and investigating on the god's behalf, if I think anyone, citizen or foreigner, is wise;
and whenever he does not seem so to me, I come to the god's aid and show that he is not wise.
And because of this occupation I have had no leisure to do anything worth mentioning for the city, or for my own household: I live in endless poverty because of my service to the god.
Besides this, the young men who follow me around of their own accord — those who have the most leisure, the sons of the richest — enjoy hearing people being examined, and they often imitate me themselves, and then try to examine others;
and then, I think, they find a great abundance of people who think they know something but know little or nothing.
As a result, those examined by them are angry with me, not with themselves, and say that there is a certain Socrates, a thoroughly loathsome man, who corrupts the young;
and whenever someone asks them what he does and what he teaches, they have nothing to say and do not know, but so as not to seem at a loss they trot out the stock charges against all philosophers: 'the things in the heavens and below the earth', 'not believing in gods' and 'making the weaker argument the stronger'.
For the truth, I think, they would not want to tell: that they are shown up as pretending to know while knowing nothing.
So since they are ambitious, I think, and vehement and numerous, and speak about me intently and persuasively, they have filled your ears, slandering me vehemently for a long time.
From among these came the attack on me by Meletus, Anytus and Lycon: Meletus aggrieved on behalf of the poets, Anytus on behalf of the craftsmen and the politicians, Lycon on behalf of the orators;
so, as I said when I began, I would be amazed if I were able to remove this slander from your minds in so short a time, when it has grown so great.
This, men of Athens, is the truth, and I tell it to you without hiding anything, great or small, or holding anything back.
And yet I know pretty well that I am making myself hated by this very thing, which is also proof that I am telling the truth, and that this is the slander against me and these are its causes.
And whether you look into this now or later, this is what you will find.
