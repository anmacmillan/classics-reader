There was once a man; as a child he had heard that beautiful story of how God tempted Abraham, and how he withstood the temptation, kept the faith and a second time received a son against expectation.
When he grew older, he read the same story with even greater admiration; for life had separated what had been united in the child's pious simplicity.
The older he grew, the more often his thought turned to that story; his enthusiasm grew stronger and stronger, and yet he could understand the story less and less.
At last he forgot everything else over it; his soul had only one wish, to see Abraham, one longing, to have been witness to that event.
His desire was not to see the beautiful regions of the East, not the earthly glory of the promised land, not that God-fearing married couple whose old age God blessed, not the venerable figure of the aged patriarch, not the vigorous youth of Isaac, bestowed by God – he would have had nothing against the same thing taking place on a barren heath.
His yearning was to go along on the 3 days' journey, when Abraham rode with sorrow before him and Isaac at his side.
His wish was to be present in the hour when Abraham lifted up his eyes and saw Mount Moriah in the distance, the hour he left the asses behind and went up alone with Isaac to the mountain; for what occupied him was not the artful weaving of the imagination but the shudder of thought.
That man was not a thinker; he felt no need to get beyond faith; it seemed to him that it must be the most glorious thing to be remembered as its father, an enviable lot to possess it, even if no one knew it.
That man was not a learned exegete; he did not know Hebrew; had he known Hebrew, then perhaps he would easily have understood the story and Abraham.
