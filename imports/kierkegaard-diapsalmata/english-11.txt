❧ Strange! with what equivocal anxiety, at losing and at keeping, man after all clings fast to this life.
At times I have thought of taking a decisive step, beside which all my previous ones were only childish pranks – of setting out on the great voyage of discovery.
As a ship, when it runs off the slipway, is greeted with cannon fire, so would I greet myself.
And yet.
Is it courage I lack?
If a stone fell down and struck me dead, that would after all be a way out.
❧ Tautology is and remains after all the highest principle, the highest axiom of thought.
What wonder, then, that most people use it.
Nor is it so poor, and it can well fill up a whole life.
It has a jesting, witty, entertaining form: these are the infinite judgments.
This kind of tautology is the paradoxical and transcendent one.
It has the serious, scientific and edifying form.
The formula for it is as follows: when two quantities are equal to one and the same third, then they are equal to each other.
This is a quantitative inference.
This kind of tautology is especially useful at lecterns and in pulpits, where one is supposed to say a great deal.
❧ The disproportion in my build is that my forelegs are too small.
Like the hare from New Holland, I have quite small forelegs, but infinitely long hind legs.
As a rule I sit quite still; if I make a movement, then it is a tremendous leap, to the horror of all those to whom I am tied by the tender bonds of kinship and friendship.
