Xerxes, after pouring libations at sunrise, waited until about the time when the marketplace is full, and then began his advance;
for this was what Ephialtes had instructed;
for the descent from the mountain is quicker, and the distance much shorter, than the way around and the climb.
So the barbarians with Xerxes came on, and the Greeks with Leonidas, as men making their march out to death, now advanced much farther than at first into the wider part of the pass.
For the defensive wall had been guarded, and on the previous days they had gone out only a little way and fought in the narrows.
But now they engaged outside the narrows, and many of the barbarians fell in great numbers;
for behind them the leaders of the companies, holding whips, flogged every man, constantly urging them forward.
Many of them fell into the sea and were drowned, and still many more were trampled alive by one another;
and no account was taken of the one who perished.
For the Greeks, knowing that death was coming to them from those who were going around the mountain, displayed against the barbarians the greatest strength they had, heedless of their lives and in a frenzy.
By this time most of them had already had their spears broken, and they were cutting down the Persians with their swords.
And in this struggle Leonidas falls, having proved himself the bravest of men, and with him other famous Spartiates, whose names I have learned, since they were worthy men; indeed I have learned the names of all three hundred.
And there too fell many Persians, among them many famous men, and in particular two sons of Darius, Abrocomes and Hyperanthes, born to Darius by Phratagune, the daughter of Artanes.
This Artanes was the brother of King Darius and the son of Hystaspes son of Arsames;
and when he gave his daughter to Darius in marriage, he gave with her his whole estate, since she was his only child.
So two brothers of Xerxes fall there fighting, and over the body of Leonidas there was a great struggle between Persians and Lacedaemonians, until the Greeks by their courage dragged it away and routed their enemies four times.
This went on until the men with Ephialtes arrived.
When the Greeks learned that these had come, from that moment the nature of the fight changed;
for they fell back into the narrow part of the road, and passing behind the wall they came and took up position on the hillock, all together in a body, all except the Thebans.
This hillock is at the entrance, where the stone lion now stands in honour of Leonidas.
In this place, as they defended themselves with daggers, those who still had them, and with hands and teeth, the barbarians buried them under their missiles, some following from the front and tearing down the defensive wall, others coming round and surrounding them on every side.
