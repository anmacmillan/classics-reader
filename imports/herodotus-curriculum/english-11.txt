While they were debating this, Xerxes sent a mounted scout to see how many they were and what they were doing.
While he was still in Thessaly he had heard that a small army had gathered there, and that its leaders were Lacedaemonians, with Leonidas, a Heraclid by descent.
When the horseman rode up to the camp, he watched and surveyed it, though not the whole camp;
for it was not possible to see those posted inside the wall, which they had rebuilt and were keeping under guard;
but he took note of those outside, whose arms lay in front of the wall;
At that moment it happened that the Lacedaemonians were posted outside.
He saw some of the men exercising, and others combing their hair.
As he watched this he was amazed, and he took note of their number.
Having learned everything precisely, he rode back at his leisure;
for no one pursued him, and he met with complete indifference;
When he got back he told Xerxes everything he had seen.
Hearing this, Xerxes could not grasp the truth, that they were preparing to die and to kill as many as they could;
instead, since their behaviour seemed to him ridiculous, he sent for Demaratus son of Ariston, who was in the camp;
When he came, Xerxes questioned him on each of these points, wanting to understand what the Lacedaemonians were doing.
Demaratus said: “You heard me speak about these men before too, when we were setting out against Greece, and when you heard me you laughed at me for telling you how I saw these things would turn out;
for to hold to the truth in your presence, O king, is my greatest struggle.
Listen now as well;
these men have come to fight us for the pass, and that is what they are preparing for.
For this is their custom;
whenever they are about to risk their lives, then they dress their heads.
Know this: if you subdue these men and those who remain behind in Sparta, there is no other race of men, O king, that will stand its ground and raise its hands against you;
for now you are attacking the noblest kingdom and city among the Greeks, and the bravest men.”
What was said seemed utterly incredible to Xerxes, and he asked a second time how men so few would fight against his army.
Demaratus said: “O king, treat me as a liar if these things do not turn out for you as I say.”
