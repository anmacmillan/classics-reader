While the king was at a loss what to do in the present situation, Ephialtes son of Eurydemus, a man of Malis, came to speak with him;
expecting to win some great reward from the king, he told him of the path that leads over the mountain to Thermopylae, and so brought about the destruction of the Greeks who had stayed there.
Later, out of fear of the Lacedaemonians, he fled to Thessaly, and while he was in exile a price in silver was put on his head by the Pylagori of the Amphictyons, when they gathered for the council at Pylae.
Some time afterwards, having returned to Anticyra, he was killed by Athenades, a man of Trachis.
This Athenades killed Ephialtes for a different reason, which I shall explain later in my account; but he was honoured by the Lacedaemonians none the less.
So Ephialtes died later on; but there is another story told, that it was Onetes son of Phanagoras, a man of Carystus, and Corydallus of Anticyra who spoke these words to the king and led the Persians round the mountain, a story I do not believe at all.
For in the first place one must judge by this: the Pylagori of the Greeks set a price in silver not on Onetes and Corydallus but on Ephialtes the Trachinian, and they surely had learned the exact truth;
and in the second place we know that Ephialtes went into exile because of this charge.
Onetes, it is true, might have known this path even though he was not a Malian, if he had spent much time in the country;
but since it was Ephialtes who led them round the mountain by the path, I record him as the one responsible.
Xerxes was pleased with what Ephialtes promised to accomplish; overjoyed at once, he sent off Hydarnes and the men Hydarnes commanded;
they set out from the camp about the time the lamps are lit.
This path had been discovered by the local Malians, and once they had found it they guided the Thessalians by it against the Phocians, at the time when the Phocians had blocked the pass with a wall and were sheltering behind it from the war;
and ever since that long-ago time the path had been shown to be of no use to the Malians.
