Since he had two older brothers, Cleomenes and Dorieus, he had put all thought of the kingship out of his mind.
But when Cleomenes died without a male heir, and Dorieus too was no longer alive but had also met his end in Sicily, then at last the kingship passed up to Leonidas, both for these reasons and because he had been born before Cleombrotus;
for Cleombrotus was the youngest son of Anaxandridas, and moreover Leonidas was married to Cleomenes' daughter.
He now marched to Thermopylae, having picked out the established three hundred, men who all happened to have sons living;
and he arrived bringing with him also those Thebans whom I counted in the total, under the command of Leontiades son of Eurymachus.
The reason why Leonidas was eager to take these alone of the Greeks with him was that they had been strongly accused of siding with the Medes;
so he summoned them to the war, wanting to know whether they would send men with him or would openly refuse the alliance of the Greeks.
They sent men, though their hearts were elsewhere.
The Spartans sent Leonidas and his men out first so that the other allies, seeing them, would take the field and not go over to the Medes as well, as they might if they learned that the Spartans were holding back;
afterwards, since the Carnea stood in their way, they intended to keep the festival, leave a garrison in Sparta, and come to help in full force with all speed.
The rest of the allies likewise intended to do much the same themselves;
for the Olympic festival happened to fall at the very same time as these events;
so, not expecting the war at Thermopylae to be decided so quickly, they sent only their advance guard.
That, then, is what they had resolved to do;
But the Greeks at Thermopylae, when the Persian came near the pass, took fright and began to debate whether to withdraw.
The other Peloponnesians thought it best to go back to the Peloponnese and guard the Isthmus;
but Leonidas, seeing the Phocians and Locrians furious at this proposal, voted to stay where they were and to send messengers to the cities telling them to come to their aid, since they were too few to beat off the army of the Medes.
