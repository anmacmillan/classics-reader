To Sardis, then at the height of its wealth, came all the other wise men of Greece who were alive at that time, each as he happened to arrive, and among them Solon, an Athenian, who had made laws for the Athenians at their request and then went abroad for ten years, sailing away on the pretext of seeing the world, so that he would not be forced to repeal any of the laws he had laid down.
For the Athenians could not do it themselves;
they were bound by solemn oaths to live for ten years under whatever laws Solon should lay down for them.
For these reasons, then, and to see the world, Solon went abroad and came to Egypt, to the court of Amasis, and then also to Sardis, to Croesus.
On his arrival he was entertained as a guest by Croesus in the royal palace;
then on the third or fourth day, at Croesus' command, servants led Solon round the treasuries and showed him that everything was great and splendid.
When he had looked at everything and examined it as he pleased, Croesus asked him the following.
“My Athenian guest, much talk has reached us about you, both for your wisdom and for your travels, how in your love of knowledge you have journeyed over much of the earth to see it;
so now a longing has come over me to ask you whether you have yet seen anyone who is the happiest of all men.”
He asked this in the expectation that he himself was the happiest of men;
but Solon, without any flattery, simply told the truth and said: “Tellus of Athens, O king.”
Croesus was astonished at what was said and asked sharply:
“On what grounds do you judge Tellus to be the happiest?”
Solon said: “Tellus, in the first place, lived when his city was prospering, and he had fine and good sons, and he saw children born to all of them and all of them survive;
in the second place, when he was well off, as we reckon things at home, the end of his life was most glorious;
for when the Athenians fought a battle against their neighbours at Eleusis, he came to the rescue, routed the enemy and died most nobly, and the Athenians buried him at public expense on the very spot where he fell and honoured him greatly.”
