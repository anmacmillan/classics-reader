So Solon gave these men second place in happiness, but Croesus grew angry and said: “My Athenian guest, is our happiness so thrown aside by you as worth nothing, that you have not even made us equal to private citizens?”
Solon said: “Croesus, you are asking about human affairs a man who knows that the divine is wholly jealous and fond of causing trouble.
For in a long span of time one may see many things one would not wish to see, and suffer many too.
I set the limit of a man's life at seventy years.
These seventy years make twenty-five thousand two hundred days, if no intercalary month is added;
but if every other year is to be made a month longer, so that the seasons may come round at the proper time, then over the seventy years there are thirty-five intercalary months, and from these months one thousand and fifty days.
Of all these days in the seventy years, which number twenty-six thousand two hundred and fifty, not one brings anything at all like another.
So then, Croesus, man is entirely a thing of chance.
