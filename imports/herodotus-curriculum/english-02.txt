When Solon had roused Croesus with his story about Tellus, telling of his many blessings, Croesus asked whom he had seen second after him, fully expecting that he would at least win second place.
Solon said: “Cleobis and Biton.
They were Argives by birth, they had a sufficient livelihood, and besides that they had bodily strength such as this:
both alike had won prizes in the games, and in particular this story is told of them.
The Argives were holding a festival for Hera, and their mother absolutely had to be brought to the temple in an ox-cart, but their oxen did not come back from the fields in time;
pressed for time, the young men put themselves under the yoke and pulled the wagon, and their mother rode on the wagon;
they drew it forty-five stades and arrived at the temple.
When they had done this and been seen by the assembled crowd, the best end of life came to them, and through them the god showed that it is better for a man to be dead than to live.
For the Argive men stood round them and praised the young men's strength, and the Argive women praised their mother for having such sons;
and the mother, overjoyed at the deed and at the praise, stood before the statue and prayed that the goddess would grant to Cleobis and Biton, her own sons, who had honoured her greatly, whatever is best for a man to obtain.
After this prayer, when they had sacrificed and feasted, the young men lay down to sleep in the temple itself and never rose again, but were held fast in that end.
The Argives had statues made of them and dedicated them at Delphi, as men who had proved themselves the best.”
