To me you seem to be very rich and to be king of many people;
but the thing you asked me, I cannot yet call you, until I learn that you have ended your life well.
For the very rich man is in no way more blessed than the one who has enough for the day, unless fortune attends him so that, with all good things still his, he ends his life well.
For many very wealthy men are unblessed, while many who have a modest livelihood are fortunate.
The man who is very rich but unblessed surpasses the fortunate man in two respects only, while the fortunate man surpasses the rich and unblessed one in many:
the one is better able to fulfil his desires and to bear a great disaster when it falls upon him, but the other surpasses him in these ways:
he is not as able as that man to bear disaster and desire, but his good fortune keeps these away from him, and he is sound in body, free from disease, untouched by troubles, blessed in his children and handsome.
And if, besides all this, he also ends his life well, this is the man you are looking for, the one who deserves to be called blessed;
but before he dies, one must hold back and not yet call him blessed, but only fortunate.
Now it is impossible for anyone, being human, to gather all these things together, just as no country is self-sufficient in providing everything for itself, but has one thing and lacks another;
and the country that has the most things, that one is the best.
So too no single human body is self-sufficient;
for it has one thing but lacks another;
but whoever continues to have the most of these things and then ends his life graciously, that man, in my view, O king, has the right to bear this name.
One must look at the end of every matter, how it will turn out;
for to many the god has given a glimpse of happiness and then overturned them root and branch.”
By saying these things he did not in any way please Croesus, who sent him away without taking any account of him, thinking him a very foolish man, since he disregarded present good things and told him to look at the end of every matter.
