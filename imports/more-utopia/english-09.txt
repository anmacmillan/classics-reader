The island of the Utopians stretches two hundred miles across its middle part — for there it is widest — and for a great part of the island is not much narrower, but towards the ends it gradually tapers on both sides.
These ends, as if drawn round with a compass over a circuit of five hundred miles, give the whole island the shape of a new moon.
Its horns are parted by a strait flowing between them, eleven miles wide more or less, which spreads out through a vast empty expanse and, since the winds are kept off by the land lying round it on every side, lies still like a huge lake rather than raging, and makes almost the whole belly of that land into a harbour.
And to men's great advantage it carries ships across in every direction.
The mouth is fearsome, with shallows on one side and rocks on the other.
Roughly in the middle of the gap one rock stands out above the water, and is therefore harmless; on it they have built a tower which they hold with a garrison; the other rocks lie hidden and treacherous.
The channels are known only to the Utopians themselves, and so it seldom happens that any foreigner gets into this bay without a Utopian pilot; indeed even for them entry is scarcely safe, unless certain marks on the shore guide their course.
If these were shifted to different places, they could easily lure an enemy fleet, however large, to its destruction.
On the other side harbours are not infrequent.
But everywhere the landing is so fortified, by nature or by art, that huge forces can be kept off from there by a few defenders.
