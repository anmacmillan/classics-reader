But at this point, lest you go astray, something must be looked at more closely.
For since they are at work for only six hours, you may perhaps think that some shortage of necessities must follow.
So far is that from happening that this time is not only enough to produce a plenty of everything required for either the necessities or the comforts of life, but even leaves some over: you too will understand this if you reckon up how large a part of the people among other nations lives in idleness.
First, almost all the women, half of the whole; or, wherever the women are busy, there, as a rule, the men snore in their stead.
Add to these how great and how idle a crowd of priests and so-called religious; add all the rich, especially the landowners, whom people commonly call gentlemen and nobles; count in with them their retinues, that whole sewer, of course, of swashbuckling good-for-nothings.
Finally, throw in the sturdy and able-bodied beggars who plead some illness as a cover for their idleness: you will certainly find those by whose labour all these things that mortals use are produced far fewer than you had thought.
For the magistrates do not drive the citizens to superfluous labour against their will, since the constitution of that commonwealth looks above all to this one aim: that, as far as public needs allow, as much time as possible should be withdrawn from the service of the body and secured for every citizen for the freedom and cultivation of the mind.
For in this, they believe, lies the happiness of life.
