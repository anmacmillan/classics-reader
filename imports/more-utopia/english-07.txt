“Yet this is not the only necessity that drives men to steal.
There is another, more peculiar to you, I believe.”
“And what is that?” said the Cardinal.
“Your sheep,” I said, “which are usually so gentle and live on so little, have now, so it is said, begun to be so greedy and wild that they devour men themselves, and lay waste and depopulate fields, houses and towns.
For in whatever parts of the realm the finer and therefore more costly wool is produced, there noblemen and gentlemen, and even some abbots, holy men, not content with the rents and annual profits that used to accrue to their ancestors from their estates, and not thinking it enough that, living in idleness and luxury, they do the public no good, unless they also do it harm,
leave nothing for the plough: they enclose everything as pasture, pull down houses, raze towns, leaving only the church to stable the sheep in; and as if too little land were wasted among you on forests and game parks, those good men turn all dwellings and every cultivated spot into a wilderness.
So, that one insatiable glutton, a dire plague to his native land, may join field to field and enclose some thousands of acres within a single fence, the tenants are thrown out.
Some are even stripped of their own, either tricked by fraud or overpowered by force, or, worn out by wrongs, are driven to sell.
And so by one means or another the wretches move out, men, women, husbands, wives, orphans, widows, parents with little children, and a household more numerous than rich, since farming needs many hands; they move out, I say, from their familiar and accustomed homes and find nowhere to take refuge; all their household goods, which would not fetch much even if they could wait for a buyer, they sell for next to nothing, since they must be thrust out.
When they have soon spent that in wandering, what finally remains for them but to steal and be hanged — justly, of course — or to wander and beg?
And yet even then they are thrown into prison as vagabonds for going about idle, when there is no one who will hire their labour, however eagerly they offer it.
For in the farm work to which they are accustomed there is nothing to be done where nothing is sown.
For one shepherd or herdsman is enough to graze with livestock that land which, to be tilled so that it could be sown, used to require many hands.”
