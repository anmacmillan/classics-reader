So it was worth seeing how they raised their crests when they compared their own finery with the dress of the Utopians — for the people had poured out into the streets.
And on the other hand it was no less a pleasure to observe how badly their hope and expectation had deceived them, and how far they fell short of the esteem they had thought they would win.
For to the eyes of all the Utopians, except a very few who had visited other nations for some good reason, all that splendid display seemed shameful; and while they respectfully greeted the lowest of the train as the lords, they took the ambassadors themselves, because they wore gold chains, for slaves, and let them pass without any honour at all.
Why, you might have seen children too, who had thrown away their gems and pearls, calling to their mothers and nudging them in the ribs when they saw such things fixed to the ambassadors' caps:
“Look, mother, what a great lout that is, still wearing pearls and little gems, as if he were a little boy!”
But the mother, quite seriously too: “Hush, son,” she said, “I think he is one of the ambassadors' fools.”
Others found fault with those gold chains as being of no use, since they were so thin that a slave could easily break them, and again so loose that he could shake them off whenever he liked and run away wherever he pleased, loose and free.
But after the ambassadors had spent a day or two there and saw so great a quantity of gold held so cheap, and treated with no less contempt than the honour they had seen it given at home, and moreover more gold and silver heaped onto the chains and fetters of a single runaway slave than the whole finery of all three of them had cost, their feathers drooped, and in shame they laid aside all that apparel in which they had so arrogantly paraded themselves.
Above all, once they had talked more familiarly with the Utopians and learned their customs and opinions: for the Utopians marvel that any mortal can take delight in the doubtful glitter of a tiny gem or pebble, when he may gaze at some star, or indeed at the sun itself, or that anyone can be so mad as to think himself nobler on account of a thread of finer wool, since this very wool — however fine its thread — a sheep once wore, and yet all the while it was nothing but a sheep.
