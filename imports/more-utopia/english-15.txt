For since they do not use money themselves, but keep it against an eventuality which, just as it may happen, so it may equally never come to pass.
Meanwhile they treat gold and silver, from which money is made, in such a way that no one values them more than their own nature deserves; and by that nature who does not see how far beneath iron they stand?
For without iron, by Hercules, mortals could no more live than without fire and water, whereas to gold and silver nature has given no use that we could not easily do without, had not human folly set a price on rarity.
On the contrary, like a most indulgent mother, she has placed all the best things out in the open, such as air, water and the earth itself, but has put vain and useless things as far away as possible.
So if these metals were locked away among them in some tower,
the prince and the senate might fall under suspicion — such is the foolish cunning of the crowd — of tricking the people by some ruse and themselves enjoying some profit from it.
Again, if they made them into bowls and other such skilfully wrought works, then if ever occasion arose that these had to be melted down again and paid out as soldiers' wages, they plainly see that people would hardly bear to have torn from them what they had once begun to count among their delights.
