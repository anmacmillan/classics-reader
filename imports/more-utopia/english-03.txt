One day, when I had attended divine service in the church of St Mary, which is both most beautiful in its workmanship and most thronged by the people, and, the mass being over, was preparing to go back from there to my lodging, I happen to see him talking with a stranger, a man verging on old age, with a sunburnt face, a long beard, and a cloak hanging carelessly from his shoulder, who from his face and dress seemed to me to be a ship's captain.
But when Peter caught sight of me, he came up and greeted me.
As I was trying to reply, he drew me a little aside and said, “Do you see this man?” — at the same time pointing out the one I had seen him talking with — “I was just now getting ready,” he said, “to bring him from here straight to you.”
“He would have been very welcome,” I said, “for your sake.”
“No,” he said, “if you knew the man, for his own.
For there is no one of all mortals alive today who could tell you so great a history of unknown peoples and lands.
And I know that you are most eager to hear of such things.”
“Then,” I said, “I did not guess too badly.
For at the first glance I sensed at once that the man was a ship's captain.”
