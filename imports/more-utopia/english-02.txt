Meanwhile I — for so the situation required — betook myself to Antwerp.
While I was staying there I was often visited, among others, but by none more welcome, by Peter Gilles, a native of Antwerp, a man of great trustworthiness and of honourable standing among his people, though worthy of the most honourable, for he is a young man of whom I cannot tell whether he is more learned or of finer character.
For he is excellent and most lettered, candid in spirit towards all besides, but towards his friends of so warm a heart, with love, loyalty and affection so sincere, that you would hardly find one or two anywhere whom you would judge his equal in every point of friendship.
Rare is his modesty; no one is further from pretence; in no one is simplicity joined with more good sense; and moreover he is so charming in conversation, and so harmlessly witty, that my longing for my country and my household hearth, my wife and children, whom I was all too anxiously eager to see again — for already by then I had been away from home more than four months — he relieved in great part by his most delightful company and his honey-sweet talk.
