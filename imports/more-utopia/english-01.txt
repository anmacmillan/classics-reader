When the most invincible King of England, Henry, the eighth of that name, adorned with every accomplishment of an outstanding prince, had lately had certain disputes on matters of no small moment with the most serene Prince Charles of Castile, he sent me as his spokesman to Flanders to discuss and settle them, as companion and colleague of that incomparable man Cuthbert Tunstall, whom he had recently made Master of the Rolls, to the great rejoicing of all;
in whose praise, indeed, I shall say nothing, not because I fear that friendship may be thought a witness of too little honest credit, but because his virtue and learning are greater than I can proclaim, and besides better known and more illustrious everywhere than to need it, unless I wish to seem, as they say, to show the sun with a lamp.
At Bruges — for so it had been agreed — we were met by those to whom the prince had entrusted the business, all of them excellent men.
Among them the governor of Bruges, a magnificent man, was the chief and head, but the mouth and heart was George Temsice, provost of Cassel, eloquent not by art alone but also by nature, most learned in the law besides, and in handling business an outstanding craftsman, both by talent and by long practice.
When we had met once and again, and could not agree well enough on certain matters, they bade us farewell for some days and set out for Brussels, to consult the prince's oracle.
